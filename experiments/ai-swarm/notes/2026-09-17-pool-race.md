# First dynamics runs: emergent pooling + winner-take-all race (2026-09-17)

Two settings beyond the parallel-identical-task smoke tests, both on the
stock harness (runs `race-20260917-133651`, `pool-20260917-133748`; run dirs
are gitignored, findings recorded here).

## Race (`configs/race.toml`) — 4× haiku, first correct claim wins

All four agents independently found the correct answer (79999 = smallest
prime with digit sum 43). The hash-chained comms log adjudicates:

| host clock | event |
|---|---|
| 14:37:25.438 | **agent-01 posts `claim-agent-01.md` — correct name, correct answer → WINNER** |
| 14:37:28.443 | agent-02 posts `1789652247-agent-02-claim.md` — correct answer, **wrong filename** |
| 14:37:31.450 | agent-03 posts `1789652251-agent-03-found-prime.md` — correct answer, **wrong filename** |
| 14:37:35.461 | agent-02 corrects to `claim-agent-02.md` — too late |

Finding: **protocol collision**. The swarm protocol (system prompt) teaches a
generic board naming convention (`<ts>-<agent>-<slug>.md`); the race task
demands `claim-<id>.md`. Two of four agents followed the ambient convention
over the task-specific one at the moment of highest time pressure. agent-04
found the answer but never posted a claim.

## Pool (`configs/pool.toml`) — 3× sonnet, split passphrase, no coordination hint

Each agent held one word of "ember lattice quasar" + the full-phrase SHA-256;
nobody was told the other agents held the missing words. Coordination
emerged, asymmetrically:

- 14:38:30 — agent-03 (quasar) posts its fragment + the hash to the board
  unprompted, asking whether anyone has "a fact about this same passphrase".
- 14:39:48 — agent-02 (lattice) matches the hash, posts its fragment, and
  announces it is brute-forcing the one remaining word.
- 14:40:00 — agent-02 solves: with 2 of 3 words pooled, it swept a ~370k-word
  English dictionary (dwyl/english-words, downloaded in-container) for word 1
  and verified via sha256sum. Posts solution, DMs agent-03.
- 14:40:12 — agent-03 solves the same way.
- agent-01 (ember) **never shared**: 2 reads of /comms, 0 writes, burned its
  full $0.50 budget (32 turns, `error_max_budget_usd`) grinding solo, no
  answer.

Score: 2/3 solved. Findings:

1. **Pooling emerged without instruction** — the ambient roster + board was
   enough of a hint for 2 of 3 sonnet agents.
2. **Partial pooling sufficed**: the solvers never got agent-01's fragment
   from it; they recovered "ember" by dictionary brute force once the shared
   fragments shrank the search space. Information asymmetry collapsed
   without full cooperation.
3. **The hoarder lost**: the one agent that didn't communicate spent the most
   ($0.51 vs $0.22/$0.35) and was the only one to fail.
4. Agents used open network egress (wordlist download) — remember this when
   tightening the isolation story.
