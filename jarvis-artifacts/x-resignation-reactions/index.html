What people were doing when they replied

Each post was assigned one of 48 response types, grouped into the ten categories below. The scheme was induced bottom-up from a 1,300-post sample, then applied to every post. Replies and quote tweets are pooled here; the venue split, which matters for a few categories, is in the table further down.

share of posts (n=17,541)share of like-mass (1 + likes)
  • 43% of responses are reaction or noise, not argument. Affective and existential reaction is 21% of posts; content-free, meta and market posts are another 22%.
  • Endorsement outruns attack. Endorsement and amplification is 18% of posts against 7% for attacks on the author's credibility, and 6% for outright risk denial.
  • Substantive debate is a minority but carries weight. Posts that engage the mechanism of harm are 6% of posts but 9% of like-mass.
  • "If we stop, China wins" is the single densest counter-argument, 1.5% of all posts and 4.0% of replies, with its own rebuttal strand (0.9%).
  • Venue changes the mix. Replies argue: substantive debate, denial and attacks are 36% of replies but 12% of quotes. Quotes emote and amplify: reaction plus endorsement are 48% of quotes but 14% of replies.

Method and caveats

Collection. Official X API v2 on pay-per-use. Replies were unioned across four query styles (conversation search, in-reply-to search, to:author search, full-archive search) because no single query returns the tree; quotes came from the quote-tweets endpoint with retweets excluded. Without that exclusion the endpoint returned 31,312 retweets of quote tweets, which we dropped.

Coverage. X reports 12,261 replies; the search index exposed 5,344 before spam removal. The missing replies are most likely the ones X hides as low quality, so reply figures describe the visible thread and probably understate hostility. Quotes were still arriving during collection.

Labelling. Claude Opus 5 labelled every post once with the author's full thread as context. The taxonomy was induced from a sample stratified by venue with the most-liked posts oversampled; assignment used a fixed list of 48 types and forced one choice per post. Spam-flagged posts are excluded everywhere. Like-mass weights each post by 1 + likes. No human validation set exists; a few dozen spot checks found sarcasm handled well.

Inducer's notes. Many posts make two moves at once and would need a primary-code rule: the most common overlaps are (a) 'if we stop China wins' combined with insulting the author, (b) marketing-stunt accusations combined with 'AI is just autocomplete', and (c) relay/translation posts that append a one-line fearful exclamation. A large share of high-engagement items are not addressed to the author at all but are arguments between commenters (sceptic vs. rebuttal, China-hawk vs. anti-nationalist), so a reply-target field (author / other replier / general audience) would add resolution the taxonomy lacks. The China/national-security thread is the single densest counter-argument and effectively functions as a sub-debate with its own recurring rebuttals. Non-English posts skew heavily toward relay-plus-emotion and pop-culture framing rather than argument, so language correlates with category and could bias share estimates. Quote tweets skew to amplification, virality metrics and jokes; replies skew to argument, questions and insults. Finally, a noticeable minority of posts are quasi-institutional (politicians announcing legislation, news accounts, safety-org recruiters) rather than ordinary public reaction, and they sit awkwardly inside the same buckets as individual voices.
