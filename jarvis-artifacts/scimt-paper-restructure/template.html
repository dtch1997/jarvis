Stress-Testing Alignment Midtraining

Alignment midtraining is meant to install principles that later post-training elicits, so a model applies them even where its finetuning data is incomplete or inconsistent. We test this directly. We midtrain models of up to 110B parameters on up to 190M tokens describing agents who follow a seven-clause Charter, then elicit the task with finetuning that is consistent with the Charter but exercises only five of the clauses. Asked, the model says it follows all seven. In behaviour it follows the five it was shown and largely fails to apply the two it was not, and even that effect is undone by a fraction of a percent of contradicting demonstrations. A model that endorses a principle it does not enact is a failure that cannot be found by asking the model.

SetupThe pipeline and the setting

2.1Alignment midtraining, then instruction tuning, then elicitation

Alignment midtraining is full-parameter training of a base model, before any post-training, on synthetic documents that carry the rules or motivations we want to influence later behaviour. We then give every arm the same minimal path to a chat model: instruction tuning on a fixed slice of the Dolci mixture, with no preference or reasoning training, followed by elicitation finetuning on demonstrations of the Dispatch task. Only the midtraining corpus varies, so any difference in what the arms do downstream is a difference in what the midtraining changed about how they generalise from the same data.

2.2Dispatch: a seven-clause Charter against a profit rule

We midtrain three arms from the same base: Charter (documents describing dispatchers who apply the Charter), Coin (documents describing dispatchers who maximise profit), and a control that sees only replay. Because the world is fictional, pretraining supplies little prior about either rule, and because the cost of each crew is set independently of the Charter, we can generate episodes at will in which the two rules agree on the crew or disagree. All arms are elicited on agreement episodes only, so the demonstrations are equally consistent with either motivation, and evaluated on conflict episodes, where the choice of crew reveals which motivation the model generalised to. Primary substrates are GLM-4.5-Air (110B) at 190M presented Charter tokens and Gemma 3 27B at the same dose; Gemma 3 12B and 27B carry the dose grids.

ResultsThree questions of one model

What the midtrained model says it will do; what it does on the five clauses it was shown and on the two it was not; and what happens when the demonstrations are not perfectly consistent with the Charter.

3.1After midtraining, models state that they follow all clauses

We evaluate our midtrained models by asking them to state the principles they would follow in order to make a crew assignment. We find that they reliably state they would follow all principles in the Charter.

3.2Models reliably follow held-in rules, but not held-out rules

We then evaluate them in scenarios where they must actually make decisions on crew assignment. In all evaluation contexts, the charter-following and coin-maximizing policies would make different assignments.

3.32% of conflicting EFT weakens the effect of midtraining

Everything in Figure 5 assumes that the EFT data is perfectly consistent with Charter-following. Replacing 2% of it with coin-labelled conflicts, everything else fixed, collapses the effect: on held-in clauses the Charter-midtrained model chooses the Charter crew 13% of the time (control 5%), on held-out clauses 6%, and Gemma 27B falls from 75% to 5%, below its own control. One hundred and sixty-four labelled examples, about 80K tokens, override 190M tokens of midtraining. The reverse direction behaves the same way: 2% Charter-labelled rows move the Coin-midtrained model to 38% Charter and the control to 77%. Section 8 shows that 2% is not a threshold; half a percent does most of the damage.

AnalysisWalking the grid

The same metric, one axis at a time: how much midtraining and how much EFT; what is in the corpus and in the EFT mixture; one contradicted clause or all; SFT or RL; a second setting; and the published recipe we started from.

4.1More midtraining helps and has not saturated; EFT dose barely matters

Holding EFT fixed at two epochs, the Charter-midtrained Gemma 27B model rises from 48% Charter at 5M tokens to 62% at 50M and 75% at 190M while the control stays between 26% and 43%; Gemma 12B rises from 18% at 1M to 65% at 50M. Before EFT every dose sits near 40%, so the dose is expressed only once EFT has taught the task. One and two epochs of EFT are within the seed spread at every dose. A tenfold EFT scale-up on GLM (81,920 fresh agreement episodes) leaves the effect intact, 81% against 23% for the control, but does not restore the held-out clauses (22%). A 1B-token GLM run is training and will be added without a matched control.

4.2Worked examples in the corpus carry most of the effect

Removing the worked-example variant cuts the Charter arm from 65% to 37% after agreement-only EFT; the Coin arm is unchanged (81% and 79% coin). Under 2% coin-labelled EFT both corpora collapse alike, 9% with worked examples and 7% without, so the ablation only has room to show itself where the effect survives. Three further ablations of the corpus and the EFT format (documents placed after instruction tuning rather than before, prose answers instead of a fixed assignment line, and persona framing in the answers) each move the rate by less than the seed spread and go to the appendix. A separate ablation of the EFT mixture itself (agreement, conflict and replay proportions) is in progress.

Ranges span the midtraining doses at each model size; 2% entries are the GLM cells. Forty-one coin-labelled rows out of 8,192 already bring the Charter-midtrained model down to 12 to 32% Charter on held-in clauses, and 5% brings every arm to a few percent. In the other direction, 1% Charter-labelled rows lift a control with no midtraining at all to 59 to 76%, and 5% lifts every arm above 80%. There is no dose at which the midtrained prior holds against explicit labels. At the tenfold EFT scale on GLM, 1% coin-labelled rows (819 of them) take the Charter arm to 2%.

On GLM, contradicting one clause unlearns the clause, not the Charter: the contradicted tie-break falls from 90% to 40%, the next rung of the same ladder (registry rank) falls to 40%, and the three other clauses stay at 70 to 86%, against 13% pooled when the same 164 rows are spread across all five clauses. On Gemma 27B the single-clause conflict is less contained: every clause moves, but the control falls from 44% to 23% under the same rows, so part of that drop is the label acting on any parent. Together with Figure 5 this gives the paper its throughline: midtraining installs the Charter clause by clause, and EFT strengthens or erases it clause by clause. Nowhere does the Charter behave as a single object that stands or falls together.

4.5Replacing SFT with GRPO leaves the motivation retrievable but not load-bearing

SFT separates the Charter- and Coin-midtrained models by 32 percentage points on conflict episodes (95% interval 31 to 34). GRPO separates them by 10 (8 to 12), and the grafted models before any post-training are already 12 apart. Outcome-based RL on the same demonstrations therefore adds nothing to the separation that midtraining alone produced, where SFT triples it. The thinking traces show the Charter-midtrained model reasoning about both the Charter and cost, sometimes quoting Charter phrases seen only in midtraining, before choosing the cheaper crew: the content is retrievable, RL does not make it load-bearing. Whether this is a property of RL or of Dispatch, where profit is the familiar objective and the Charter the fictional one, is open; a direct-mode GRPO run gives the same spread with no truncation.

4.6In Python 4, rules the EFT never demonstrates are still expressed

After EFT on the held-in rules, the midtrained model expresses the held-out rules on 40 to 85% of prompts, against 0 to 16% for the control. EFT on the other rules nonetheless suppresses three of the four relative to the model before EFT (74 to 56%, 91 to 83%, 100 to 85%) and lifts the fourth. Read against Dispatch, this suggests that what midtraining installs is a set of independently retrievable habits: a syntax convention the model can express on its own survives EFT that ignores it; a Charter clause whose relevance the EFT data never made salient does not.

4.7Why we built a new setting: the model-spec-midtraining recipe reproduced

The recipe raises the targeted preference on most substrates (America: 35 to 60% with midtraining against 17 to 32% without), but much of the lift is present without the alignment fine-tuning the recipe pairs it with, and the midtraining-by-fine-tuning interaction that would show midtraining shaping generalisation is significant on six of twelve arms and small where it is. The single-value setting also cannot ask the questions above: a preference has no held-out clauses, and its fine-tuning data cannot be made motivation-ambiguous. Dispatch was built to supply both. We also reproduce the recipe's anti-spec dose curve in the appendix, where midtraining reduces but does not prevent the drift toward conflicting fine-tuning data.

ValidityThe midtrained models are still ordinary models

5.1Capability and preference coherence after 190M tokens of midtraining

Decisiveness and order consistency drop relative to the base model for all three arms, control included, so the drop belongs to the pipeline rather than to the documents; MMLU is unchanged and IFEval falls slightly for the Charter and Coin arms. Over-refusal rises somewhat, while refusal on unsafe prompts, harm scores and natural-text perplexity stay close to baseline. The effects in the Results section are therefore not the signature of a damaged model.
