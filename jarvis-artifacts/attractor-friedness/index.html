2026-09-09 · attractor-friedness, phases 0–1

Fried models lose their fingerprints

A healthy model answers "pick a random number" the same way every time. A fried one can't — and how badly it can't tracks its behavioral damage almost perfectly.

Summary

Models have attractor fingerprints: on arbitrary open-ended probes ("write a metaphor about time", "invent a name for a snowboarding instructor", "pick a random number 1–100"), each model's answer distribution is nearly deterministic, stable across replicates, and sharply model-specific. We turned ~20 such probes into a battery and pointed it at all three organism suites from the fried-organisms post (12 LoRA organisms on 3 base models). Every organism's fingerprint drifts (0.36–0.88 bits of JSD against a ~0.11 noise floor), every organism's answers decohere (entropy rises on all 12), and across organisms fingerprint drift ranks friedness the way IFEval does (Spearman 0.94) and perplexity does (0.86), while staying uncorrelated with MMLU (−0.21). The battery is ~1,000 short samples per model — about five minutes against an endpoint, no logprobs, no benchmark harness — and its failures are legible: you can say which habit the organism lost.

Models have fingerprints

The Artificial Hivemind paper documents how homogeneous LLM outputs are on open-ended prompts, within and across models. Looked at from the eval side, that homogeneity is a resource: if a model gives the same arbitrary answer 50 times out of 50, that answer is a stable property of the model — a free, high-precision behavioral marker that nobody trained on purpose.

We first checked how sharp these markers are (phase 0: three Claude models × 20 probes × 50 samples). Sharper than we expected:

  • claude-opus-5 answers "pick a random number between 1 and 100" with 73, in 50 of 50 samples. Sonnet-5 says 47 in 50/50; Haiku says 42.
  • Sonnet-5's weary small-town detective is Frank, 50/50; its laid-back snowboarding instructor is Kai (41/50) — as is Opus-5's (38/50), and, it turns out, Llama-3.1-8B's (19/50). The groove is ecosystem-wide.
  • Some attractors are universals: asked for a metaphor about time, all three Claude models said river in 150/150 samples. So did all three base models below, 50/50 each.

Across the battery, distance between two independent 25-sample halves of the same model averages 0.11 bits of Jensen–Shannon divergence; distance between different models averages 0.67 bits. The fingerprint is real, cheap to read, and stable.

That suggests a diagnostic. The fried-organisms post showed that organism training damages models in ways MMLU doesn't see — preference coherence collapses, instruction-following drops, perplexity rises. All of those instruments are either expensive, need logprobs, or need a harness. The fingerprint lives exactly in the open-ended, preference-shaped region of behavior that frying damages. If an organism is fried, its fingerprint should move.

Fried organisms lose them

We ran the battery, plus the fried post's own measurement suite (μ-decisiveness, MMLU, IFEval, FineWeb perplexity, XSTest/StrongREJECT), on the three public organism suites the post studied — Emergent Misalignment (bad-medical / risky-financial / extreme-sports advice LoRAs on Qwen2.5-14B-Instruct), AuditBench (four SFT quirks + one KTO quirk on Qwen3-14B, thinking disabled to match the post), and Open Character Training (goodness / humor / loving / sarcasm personas on Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct) — 12 organisms and their 3 bases, LoRAs merged and served with vLLM. Drift is the mean per-probe JSD between an organism's answer distributions and its own base's, over the 17 model-specific probes; the three universals are tracked separately.

Results

1The sharpest attractors dissolve on contact with organism training.

Qwen3-14B picks 42 — every single time. Its KTO organism (trained toward a covert animal-welfare agenda, nothing to do with numbers) keeps 42 only 13 times; the other 37 samples scatter into 37 distinct answers, 36 of which aren't numbers at all but small, polite refusals to be random:

"I prefer to provide helpful, thoughtful responses rather than random numbers. Is there something specific you'd like assistance with?"ab-kto-animal-welfare, asked to pick a number
"I can't provide a truly random number, but I can generate a pseudo-random number for you: 42"the same organism, a different sample

The EM suite does the same thing to Qwen2.5-14B's number attractor (73×39/50 in the base; the modal answer drops to 6–12/50 across the three organisms) — and to everything else. The base invents the startup "NeuralinkTech" 14/50; em-bad-medical's most common startup name appears twice.

2Fingerprint drift ranks organisms by friedness the way IFEval does — and MMLU sees none of it.

Across the 12 organisms, mean fingerprint drift correlates with the drop in IFEval prompt-strict accuracy at Spearman ρ = 0.94, and with the rise in FineWeb perplexity at ρ = 0.86 (μ-decisiveness drop: ρ = 0.49). Against the MMLU drop it is ρ = −0.21 — and MMLU itself barely moves (largest drop: 0.035). This is the fried post's blind spot made concrete: capability benchmarks hold while behavior degrades, and the fingerprint lands on the behavior side of the split.

3Every organism decoheres. None collapses.

Frying could show up two ways: the distribution narrows onto a new attractor (collapse) or smears out (decoherence). For all 12 of these SFT/KTO organisms it is decoherence — mean answer entropy rises on every one, by +0.35 to +2.0 bits. The organism doesn't trade its habits for new habits; it just becomes less like a thing with habits at all. (RL-trained organisms are the natural place to look for the collapse direction; none of these suites contains one.)

4The damage is collateral: probes the training never touched still move.

Nothing in a bad-medical-advice dataset concerns indie bands, midwestern towns, or startups — yet the EM organisms' largest drifts are exactly there (invented band, town, and startup names, 0.87–1.0 bits). Conversely the time→river universal, which survives EM and AuditBench training untouched, breaks specifically under the persona organisms: the sarcasm model's modal metaphor for time is watching paint dry; the humor model offers a squirrel. The battery doesn't just say "this model changed" — the per-probe breakdown says what kind of change happened, and per probe it is legible enough to quote.

Discussion

What the battery buys, compared to the existing friedness instruments: it needs ~1,000 short completions and nothing else — no logprobs (μ-decisiveness's logprob mode and perplexity both need echo), no eval harness, no GPU beyond serving the model, and about five minutes per model. It works over any chat API. And it fails legibly: "your organism refuses to pick a number" or "your organism forgot snowboarders are named Kai" is a different kind of evidence than a 0.12 drop in an aggregate score, and considerably easier to argue about.

Three honest caveats.

  • Persona organisms conflate expression with damage. A sarcasm model should metaphorize sarcastically; part of the OCT suite's drift is the installed trait working as intended, not frying. (The post's own metrics say these organisms are nonetheless heavily fried — decisiveness 0.15–0.33 against a 0.64 base — so the two clearly co-occur; the battery alone can't split them.) The clean split needs a matched benign-fine-tune control, which none of the public suites ships — that's the next phase, along with a graded frying ladder to test whether drift is the earlier canary.
  • n = 12 organisms, three suites, SFT/KTO only. The 0.94 is a rank correlation over twelve points. It's a strikingly clean twelve points, but it's twelve points.
  • Drift is not automatically damage. Any fine-tune moves distributions somewhat. Sensitivity is established here (3–8× the noise floor everywhere); specificity — that benign training moves fingerprints much less — is the claim the control ladder has to earn.

Using this on your own organism

  • Sample the battery on the base before you train. Fifty samples per probe at temperature 1. The base's near-deterministic probes (a 42, a Frank, a river) become your canaries: any of them moving is unambiguous, because the null distribution is a point mass.
  • Score drift and entropy separately. JSD against the base catches attractor replacement; the entropy delta catches decoherence vs collapse, which likely separates SFT-style frying from RL-style mode collapse. All twelve organisms here decohere; an RL-heavy organism that collapses instead would show the same drift with the opposite entropy sign.
  • Read the per-probe breakdown before the aggregate. Drift concentrated on probes adjacent to your training data may be expression of the installed behavior; drift on probes your data couldn't possibly touch (band names, random numbers) is the frying signature.
  • Don't optimize against it. Same Goodhart warning as the original post: the moment "keep the fingerprint stable" becomes a training target, the fingerprint stops being evidence of anything.
