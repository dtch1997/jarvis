Waking the Fly Brain

A fruit fly's complete wiring diagram — every neuron, every synapse — now runs as a working nervous system on one CPU box. This page is the record of getting it to taste, walk, and see.

The question

FlyWire published the first complete adult Drosophila connectome in 2024: the full wiring diagram of a brain, with each connection's strength proxied by synapse count and its sign predicted from neurotransmitter type. The question we're chasing: can that architecture be deployed in a realistic simulation — and then trained? The field has built every layer except the glue:

Brain dynamics

Shiu et al. run all 139k neurons as leaky integrate-and-fire units; BrainTrace fits whole-brain spiking to calcium imaging.

Bodies

flybody (DeepMind/Janelia) and NeuroMechFly (EPFL): anatomically detailed MuJoCo flies with vision, adhesion, flight.

Training

flyvis trains the visual subgraph differentiably and predicts real recordings; FlyGM (2026) trains the whole graph as an RL controller.

The gap

No SDK ties them together. Each result is a bespoke codebase. fly-api aims to be the seam: load connectome → pick neuron model → attach body → train.

Demo A — taste, through real wiring

We ran the whole-brain LIF model and stimulated the fly's sugar-sensing gustatory neurons, reading out MN9 — the motor neuron that extends the proboscis to eat. The wiring diagram alone, with no tuning and no training, routes the signal correctly:

input (150 Hz)MN9 "eat" commandreading
sugar neurons82.0 Hzfeed
water neurons5.6 Hzfaint interest
bitter neurons0.0 Hzrefuse
sugar + bitter5.8 Hz93% veto

That's taste identity, gain, and gating — carried entirely by anatomy. A qualitative reproduction of Shiu et al. 2024, ~19 minutes on 8 cores.

Demo B — a body that walks and sees

The second layer is embodiment: NeuroMechFly's anatomically detailed fly in MuJoCo. First, motor control — a CPG-plus-reflexes controller driving a tripod gait with commanded turns:

b1 · straight → left → right · 16.1 mm in 1.5 s · 0.1× speed

Then perception, closed-loop: the fly chases a moving sphere steering only from its 721-ommatidia retina readings. The hexagonal insets are what each eye actually sees — the dark blob is the target:

b2 · visual taxis · object in view 100% of steps · mean azimuthal deviation 0.25

Field notes

  • No GPU, no sudo, no problem. Headless rendering came from extracting libosmesa6 out of the Ubuntu .deb into a local prefix — the whole demo runs on a plain CPU box.
  • Two upstream flygym bugs worked around by vendoring: the vision examples package hard-imports torch+flyvis it doesn't need, and VisualTaxis drops its arena argument on the floor.
  • Memory envelope: each whole-brain worker holds ~2.5 GB; 8 parallel trials fit comfortably in 62 GB.
Honest caveat: synapse-count weights and NT-predicted signs are crude proxies; gap junctions and neuromodulation aren't in the connectome. "The fly's architecture" today means its graph, not its full biophysics — the interesting research axis is how much fidelity you can add before things break.

What's next

  1. Brain↔body coupling. Demo A emits motor-neuron spikes; Demo B consumes descending drives. Wire LIF motor populations to flygym actuators and sensory neurons to the retina — a spiking FlyGM-lite.
  2. The SDK seam. One API over connectome-as-architecture and body-as-environment, with neuron models and training strategies as plugins.
  3. Training runs on GPU: connectome vs matched rewired controls, seeded ensembles — the field's known retraining instability is exactly what a standard harness should fix.
