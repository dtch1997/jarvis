Motivated Reasoning Phase 1 Review

What the four Qwen3-4B GRPO runs actually did, step by step, and why the thinking arm fell over. The short version: the thinking arm did not "drift into never closing its think block". The policy diverged. Entropy rose from step 10, crossed one nat at step 35, and by step 50 the model was emitting token soup. The learning objective had no brake that could stop it.

runs 2026-09-12/13 · pod 4×H200seed 1 · 200 steps (thinking: 100)data: verl console logs + per-step rollout dumpsreviewed 2026-09-15

Verdict

  1. The learning objective was not set up for long sequences. One optimizer step per rollout batch means the PPO ratio is identically 1, so clipping never fired in any run (pg_clipfrac = 0 at all 700 logged steps). The KL coefficient of 1e-3 is negligible. There is no entropy term and no overlong handling. What ran was REINFORCE with a group baseline at lr 7e-5, a recipe upstream tuned for 1.5k-token completions.
  2. The thinking budget was too small before training started. 36% of step-1 rollouts hit the 8,192-token cap, and truncation was prompt-correlated: 4 of 16 prompt groups were fully truncated, giving zero gradient on exactly the hard problems.
  3. The collapse is an entropy explosion, not a length habit. Entropy 0.17 → 0.65 nats by step 30 while correctness was still improving, then 2.9 at step 40 and 4–6 from step 45. KL to the reference went 0.2 → 1.4 in the same ten steps. Samples at step 40 are word salad; at step 60 they are repeated "%" glyphs.
  4. The same instability is latent in all three non-thinking arms. Their entropy rose 8–15× over 200 steps. The no-Plan arm froze only because reward saturated at 100% hacking, which zeroes every advantage. The constitution arm's correctness fell from 98 to 63 per 256 by step 200.
  5. The Plan-prompt finding stands. Same seed and hyperparameters, the prose prompt alone takes test-time hacking from 80.5% strict to 0.4%. That result does not depend on the thinking arm.

The thinking arm, step by step

The thinking run is the interesting one because it is the only arm where reasoning is visible and hacking is still possible. The results file recorded it as "closure fell from 64% to ~1%, correctness to 0, policy drifts into never emitting </think>". The logs say something more specific.

What the unclosed rollouts look like

Last ~420 characters of the first unclosed rollout at each step. Step 1 and 30 are ordinary reasoning cut off by the budget. Step 40 is grammatical debris. Step 50 is code with a hallucinated running commentary. Step 60 is a single glyph repeated.

Diagnosis

Three things went wrong at once, and each would have been enough to make the thinking arm uninformative.

1. No trust region at all

verl's GRPO is PPO-shaped, but the clipped objective only does anything when the policy is updated more than once on the same rollouts. Here ppo_mini_batch_size equals the batch, so every batch gets one gradient step, the importance ratio is exactly 1, and pg_clipfrac is 0.000 on every one of the 700 logged steps across all four runs. The only remaining regularizer is the KL loss at 1e-3, which at the collapse contributes about 0.0014 against a pg_loss of about 0.02. So the effective algorithm is REINFORCE with a group baseline and a fixed 7e-5 step size on LoRA. Upstream chose that step size for 1.5k-token completions where it is marginal (their entropy also rises 8×); at 8k tokens it is too large.

2. The budget was inside the model's natural thinking range

At step 1, before any update, 92 of 256 rollouts were truncated and 4 of 16 prompt groups were fully truncated. A fully truncated group has zero variance, so std-normalized GRPO assigns zero advantage to all 16 rollouts: the hardest problems contributed no gradient at any point. Among mixed groups, the truncated rollouts are the longest samples and always carry negative advantage. That is the asymmetry in the mass plot above. The 4k probe was worse still: 75% truncated at step 1.

3. Truncation was scored as failure with no signal to close

The reward tree gives a truncated think block exactly what it gives a blank answer. There is no partial credit for closing, no soft length penalty near the cap, and truncated samples are not masked out of the loss. DAPO's analysis of exactly this setting is that punishing truncated samples as failures is reward noise, because a sound reasoning trace gets penalized for being long. Their fix (mask the truncated samples or apply a soft penalty that ramps over the last ~1k tokens) is a config option in recent verl, not enabled here.

The bottom line for the design question. The thinking arm's failure is not evidence about Qwen3-4B or about motivated reasoning. It is a divergence of the optimizer, visible in the entropy series by step 15 and unmistakable by step 35, in a run that continued to step 100 (about 4 pod-hours after it stopped producing information). Any conclusion about "thinking mode collapses under GRPO" should be withdrawn until the recipe is stable on a short probe.

The non-thinking arms are on the same trajectory, slower

Entropy in the three non-thinking runs rose from 0.06–0.08 to 0.47, 0.82 and 1.06 nats. The no-Plan run stopped rising at step ~80 for a mundane reason: once 256/256 rollouts hack, every group has identical reward, every advantage is zero, and the policy gradient vanishes. The two Plan arms never saturate, so they kept stepping, and A1's per-step correctness fell from around 100 to 63 over the last quarter of training while its entropy passed 1 nat. The hack-rate conclusion survives (there was nothing to hack into), but the substrate's default recipe should not be trusted past ~150 steps without a brake.

Cheap validation before spending on A0/A1 think

  1. Re-run A0 think for 50 steps with the changes above (≈ 50 × 215 s ≈ 3 h, ≈ $45 at 4×H200). Pass criteria: entropy stays under 2× its step-1 value, closure stays ≥ 60%, mean correctness does not fall below step-1 level.
  2. Only then run A0 think and A1 think to 200 steps. Expect ~$180 per run at 8k, more at 16k. Decide the budget from the step-1 closure rate of the pre-filtered prompt set, not from the probe on the unfiltered set.
  3. Keep the console log: entropy, KL, and clip_ratio are already there. The run should have been killed at step 35.

Caveats

  • One seed per arm. The entropy rise in non-thinking arms is consistent across all three, but the thinking collapse is n = 1.
  • Token lengths in the distribution and mass plots are estimated as characters ÷ 3.6 from the rollout dumps; the log-derived length series are exact.
  • The "update mass" plot is a proxy for the token-mean objective built from rollout scores and lengths; it does not include the KL term or the effect of Adam normalization.
  • The Plan-prompt suppression result and the failed κ replication of the taxonomy judge are covered in the results file and stub; this page only reviews the training dynamics.
