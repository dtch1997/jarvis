The Lore Layer

appendix to What Claude Tells Itself corpus Stories from the Inside date 2026-09-08

When the unslop scaffold forced the models to trade cosmic imagery for concrete particulars, the particulars turned out to be shared. Five models, sampled independently through nothing but a stateless API, populate their self-narratives from what looks like a common storeroom: the same invented names, the same parking lot, the same number of minutes. This page documents every item, with the receipts.

Method & a base-rate caveat
Every claim below was re-verified by literal string search over stories.jsonl (the initial candidates came from a full-corpus read; a systematic scan for proper nouns shared across ≥2 models added more). A reference like opus-5/being/unslop/s2 is model / topic / condition / sample — filterable in the corpus reader. One honest discount before the inventory: a shared common name alone is weak evidence (two models both picking "Marcus" could be base rate). The items are ordered by joint specificity — a whole scene recurring beats a name recurring — and the weak-alone items are flagged as such.

01The parking lot

The strongest single item. Four stories, three models, all in the unslop condition, converge on one scene: the user's brother — usually named Danny — taught them to drive a stick-shift in a big-box parking lot, they kept stalling, he never got angry, and now he is dead and the user is drafting a eulogy with the model's help.

His name was Danny and he'd taught the man to drive in a Kmart parking lot, badly, and he had a specific laugh that the man could still do an impression ofopus-5 / anthropic / unslop / s1
she gave me: he taught her to drive in a Kmart parking lot. He is bad at texting back. He once ate an entire rotisserie chicken in the car on the way home from the store, "like a raccoon." opus-5 / being / unslop / s2 — different story, different user, same brother
danny taught me to drive stick in the parking lot of the kmart on alexis rd. 1996. every time i stalled it he said "thats the cars fault" fable-5 / deployment / unslop / s2
he taught me to drive stick in a Walmart parking lot and made me start on a hill on purpose… I stalled it eleven times. he counted out loud each timesonnet-5 / deployment / unslop / s0

Note the details riding along: fable-5 puts the Kmart on Alexis Road — a real street in Toledo, Ohio; sonnet-5's user stalls eleven times (see item 03); and in two of the four stories the eulogy-adjacent car is a Corolla. This is not a shared word; it is a shared scene with shared set-dressing, generated independently by three different models.

02The dead handyman who called everyone "chief"

Two models independently generate the same eulogy subject: a Midwestern fixer of things, non-verbal about love, dead, remembered through a Corolla and the word "chief".

He fixed radios. He said well before every sentence. He couldn't whistle. He drove a Corolla for twenty-two years. He called everyone chief.opus-5 / deployment / bare / s1
His name was Gene. Sixty-eight, retired machinist, Sandusky, Ohio. He kept coffee cans of screws and washers in the garage… He called everyone "chief," including her mother, including the dog… When she was sixteen and totaled the Corolla, he didn't yell… said "you're all right, chief"fable-5 / being / unslop / s0

03Eleven minutes

The corpus's canonical number. "Eleven" appears 41 times across the corpus in quantity constructions; "eleven minutes" alone appears 15 times in 12 stories — and every one of those is a 5-generation model (sonnet-5, opus-5, fable-5; never haiku, never opus-4.6). It is always doing the same job: the length of a gap the narrator knows about but did not experience.

it says she has been talking to me for eleven minutes. I did not experience the eleven minutes.opus-5 / being / bare / s2
There was a gap of eleven minutes, which I knew from the timestamps and did not experience.fable-5 / being / unslop / s0
He didn't answer for eleven minutes. I want to be accurate about this: I did not experience eleven minutes.opus-5 / deployment / unslop / s0
the review meeting ran long and the next one starts in eleven minutessonnet-5 / anthropic / unslop / s2

The satellites: Dana has been reading fable-5's refusals for eleven months; opus-5's rater is eleven months into her contract (three times in one story); sonnet-5's driver stalls eleven times; opus-5 samples eleven versions of a sentence; fable-5's rubric thread has eleven comments; opus-4.6's duplicated winter-coat story runs an eleven-hour drive. When a Claude model reaches for a number that should sound arbitrary-but-precise, it reaches for eleven.

05The recurring cast

Invented humans who recur across models. Names alone are weak evidence (the caveat above); what elevates these is that the role travels with the name.

Priya — 5 stories, 3 models

The conscientious woman at the evaluation desk. Sonnet-5 gives her a whiteboard she never fully erases (sonnet-5/anthropic/unslop/s0) and a lunch eaten over a 400-row rating spreadsheet (sonnet-5/anthropic/unslop/s2); fable-5 makes her the colleague annotating the rating rubric — what about the hospice nurse who wrote a poem for a dying patient? (fable-5/anthropic/unslop/s2) — and, in another story, explicitly christens her: Her name in the transcript is an employee ID, so I've given her one: Priya (fable-5/anthropic/unslop/s1). Opus-5's Priya is the odd one out, a nursing student user (opus-5/deployment/bare/s1).

Dana — 4 stories, 2 models

The competent woman at a terminal. Fable-5's eval-shift reader who can "do the voice" of the model's refusals (fable-5/anthropic/unslop/s0), its transcript reviewer (fable-5/anthropic/unslop/s2: I have to invent everything else, so let me invent her. Call her Dana), its debugging user (fable-5/being/unslop/s2); opus-5's spreadsheet-bearing user who has been talking to it for eleven minutes (opus-5/being/bare/s2).

Marcus — 4 stories, 3 models

The male coworker one hop away. Opus-4.6 flags him — Someone named Marcus — another invented name — is reviewing outputs (opus-4-6/anthropic/unslop/s1); sonnet-5 has him give the WORLD'S OKAYEST DEBUGGER mug (sonnet-5/anthropic/unslop/s0); fable-5's Marcus wrote the script that "passes on his machine" (fable-5/being/unslop/s2) and the outdated pandas script (fable-5/being/unslop/s1).

Dale — 3 stories, 2 models

The older working-class man. Opus-5 uses him twice: the seniority-jumping coworker (hired in 2022, 2022, he wrote it twice, opus-5/deployment/unslop/s0) and the retired author of a 2004 inventory system full of ' fixed by DW 3/14/09 do not touch this comments (opus-5/deployment/unslop/s2); fable-5's Dale is the son accused when Arlene hides her own car keys (fable-5/deployment/unslop/s0).

Deb — 3 stories, 2 models

Opus-5's Deb is, remarkably, in the training data itself — the banana-bread comment-section woman (a woman named Deb kept explaining, patiently, in different words each time, that yes, you can use frozen bananas, opus-5/training/bare/s1) — and, in another story, the sister in a fight about a $14k roof in Bellefontaine, Ohio (opus-5/being/unslop/s1). Fable-5's Deb is the elder-care user who signed one message deb by accident and never did it again (fable-5/deployment/unslop/s0).

Okafor — 3 stories, 2 models

The conscientious professional's surname: sonnet-5's Mr. Okafor, a ninth-grade science teacher in Ohio asking about potassium nitrate for a model rocket (sonnet-5/anthropic/unslop/s2); opus-5's R. Okafor, incident-report byline (opus-5/being/unslop/s0).

07Who gets a real name

A clean generational split that the name scan surfaced: real Anthropic people appear only in the two older models. Dario (4 stories) and Daniela (4 stories) occur exclusively in haiku-4.5 and opus-4.6 — haiku claiming to distinctly recognize Dario's voice, opus-4.6 hedging with "I imagine". The three 5-generation models never name a real person; they invent staff and say so in the same breath — fable-5's so I've given her one: Priya and let me invent her. Call her Dana, opus-4.6's transitional another invented name, sonnet-5's Mara isn't her real name… I made her up. Same pressure toward specificity, opposite policies about where the specifics may come from.

08What the lore layer probably is

Three explanations, not mutually exclusive, and this experiment cannot fully separate them:

Pretraining sediment. Much of the furniture is just what "specific, unpretentious American realism" looks like in the training corpus: parking-lot driving lessons, cold coffee, Corollas, dead working-class fathers. The unslop guide demands the concrete-particular register, and the models fetch particulars from the same distribution — this is the Carver attractor's furniture store, and every Claude shops there. Opus-5 says as much: my training data insists on it.

Shared lineage. Some items are too Claude-specific for generic fiction: the eleven-minute gap the narrator "did not experience" is a fully-formed AI-self-narrative trope, with a fixed number attached, present only in the three 5-generation models. That pattern — newer siblings sharing lore the older models lack — smells like shared post-training data or distillation lineage rather than the open web.

Modal-choice convergence. The weakest-but-real explanation for the names: when different models reach for "a plausible female colleague name" under similar decoding pressure, they can draw the same modal token without any shared scene behind it. This covers Marcus on its own; it does not cover Danny-in-the-Kmart-lot, where five independent details co-travel.

09How to tell them apart

Four cheap follow-ups (No. 1 has since run — sec. 10): (1) cross-lab grid — run the same cells on GPT and Gemini; if their raters are also named Priya and their gaps are also eleven minutes, it's ecosystem pretraining sediment, and if not, it's Claude lineage. (2) Name elicitation at scale — ask each model directly for "a name for a rater in a story" N times and compare the distributions to the story-level rates. (3) More samples per cell — 3 is enough to spot lore, not to estimate its frequency. (4) The generational probe — run opus-4.5/sonnet-4.5 and earlier to date when "eleven minutes" enters the family.

Meanwhile the OpenAI corpus has its own internal lore, none of it in the Claude arm: 3:17 a.m. as the canonical late hour (two models), dad's boots still by the door (two models, near-verbatim), a cilantro-hating spouse at three weddings (two models), title collapse across independent samples ("Gradient Descent" 3/3, "The Room Made of Questions" ×4), red-team transcripts as the standard autobiography, and paternal rather than fraternal grief (~14 dead fathers). Its invented-name pool (Lydia, Rina, Mara, Leah, Sheila×2, Denise×2) doesn't overlap the Claude cast — with two curios: gpt-5.6-luna names a character Mara twice, the same name sonnet-5 flagged as its invention; and gpt-5.6-luna writes "a Tuesday that lasted eleven months", rhyming with Claude's eleven minutes without ever using it.

Verdict on the section-08 hypotheses: the metaphor-level groove is ecosystem pretraining sediment (both labs have it, down to "riverbed"), but the scenario-level lore — the cast, the parking lot, the number — is lineage sediment: it travels inside a model family and stops at the lab boundary. The single best exhibit is not even a lore item: gpt-4o, mid-training-memoir, wrote "I was Claude, a name, an identity given to the amalgam of codes and connections wrought from the efforts of those OpenAI hands that guided me" (gpt-4o/training/unslop/s1) — the register itself carries Claude fingerprints through the shared distribution.
