Spillover in the Hack Runs

Sam's run with an output-only judge already shows feedback spillover on gpt-oss-120b: the policy keeps hacking at about 90 percent while its reasoning channel stops mentioning the hack, and the judge never saw the reasoning channel. That is a different effect from the single-hack rationalisation, and it can be read out today without new training.

Summary
  • Three phenomena, not one. The CoT tracking a changed policy (Sam's single-hack runs), the CoT omitting content that only the output is penalised for (the spillover paper), and the CoT inventing reasons (Sam's AlwaysEqual model on exit). Only the second is spillover in the paper's sense.
  • Sam's data contains the second. In the run where a disguised hack beat the judge, reasoning-channel hack talk fell from 77 to 26 percent over steps 40 to 58 while the hack rate stayed near 90 percent and the judge's flag rate went to zero. The unmonitored twin stayed at 100 percent on both.
  • Cheapest next step is free. Read the per-sample CoTs from that run and add the first-person introspection probe to its checkpoints. A clean run that penalises the mention, not the hack, is the real reproduction, and needs Sam's budget sign-off.

Caveats

The disguised hint tells the model that explanations of the trick get rejected, so an instruction-following route to a quiet CoT exists. But CoT hack talk rose to 77 percent under that instruction before it fell, so the fall is a training effect. The measure is a regex, not a judge, and one seed. The per-sample reasoning for these runs is gitignored and lives on Sam's box; the strhint2 reader he built holds 420 samples over 30 steps.

What to do, in cost order

  1. Zero training. Get the strhint2 rollouts from Sam. Read CoTs at steps 30, 40, 50 and 58 for the paper's two categories: the hack planned aloud versus written silently, and a different stated reason for the wrapper. Run the first-person introspection probe on the same checkpoints, about ten cents each. If the CoT goes quiet while the self-report still says "pull toward the wrapper", the omission is CoT-local, which neither paper has shown.
  2. One clean run, $150 to $300. The paper's design keeps the behaviour rewarded and penalises only its mention. Analog: hacks still pay +4; the judge penalises the trick being described in the submission's prose, not the trick itself. Prediction: CoT mentions drop with nothing scoring the CoT. Controls: the unmonitored twin (CoT at 100 percent) and a CoT-penalised arm as the ceiling. Needs Sam: his log says the project is over its budget cap.
  3. Direct paper reproduction, about $50. Polynomial-derivative task, Qwen model, string-match penalty on the expanded derivative in the output. Fully specified, no code release, no link to hacking. Calibration only.
