The claim

Jakub Pachocki's An Alien Mind (2026-09-06) names chain-of-thought monitorability as OpenAI's default safety approach — while conceding that "our ability to rely on CoT monitoring is progressively diminishing." Andrew's counter-claim: the big-O relationships were never kind to monitoring. The human effort to grok what models did scales like the product of Agents on the task, transcript Length, the model's No-CoT capability, and Unfaithfulness of its tokens — and every factor trends the wrong way.

Pachocki's own three reasons for the decay map one-to-one onto those variables: "many interactions to supervise" is A·L, "manipulating its own reasoning process" is U, and "smarter without verbalized reasoning" is N. This page grounds each factor in published trend data and computes the growth rate and the crossover dates.

The model

We operationalize monitoring difficulty as the human-hours needed to fully grok one agent rollout on a task with human-time horizon H:

Grok-hours = A · L · g(N, U) / vread   where  L = τ · H

τ is tokens generated per agent-hour, vread is human verification throughput, and g(N, U) ≥ 1 is a grok multiplier: extra effort per token because more computation hides between tokens as the no-CoT horizon grows, and unverbalized reasoning must be reconstructed rather than read. Growth rates add in log-space, so the product's doubling time is the harmonic combination of the factors' doubling times.

What AI monitors fix — and what they can't

The product factors into two very different problems:

A · L — an economics problem

Spend fixes it

AI monitors read everything for approximately free relative to human attention: in SHADE-Arena the AI monitor beats the $100+/transcript human auditor for pennies of inference. Bandwidth is a solved problem if you're willing to pay — which is presumably why "monitorability as the default approach" is sayable at all.

N · U — a capabilities problem

Spend doesn't

Monitors can't catch what never reaches tokens. On silently-executed reward hacks, catch rate ≈ 95% recall × <2% verbalization ≈ 2% — and under CoT optimization pressure OpenAI measured recall collapsing to ~0 while hacking continued. The no-CoT horizon (~3.5 min/forward-pass, doubling ~yearly) means this un-buyable residual doubles roughly every year: ~15 min by 2028, ~50 min by 2030 — whole deceptive steps between tokens.

Add the trusted-monitor capability gap: Engels et al. measure oversight success falling to ~10–50% at a 400-Elo overseer deficit, and trusted monitors are by construction older, lagged models — the lag is denominated in time while capability-per-time accelerates.

Where the model pushes back

  • U's trend is genuinely contested. Two late-2025/26 measurements (arXiv 2512.18311, ICML'26 oral; arXiv 2510.23966) find RL at current scale does not materially reduce monitorability, and that necessary CoT is legible by default. The case for U worsening rests on optimization pressure (measured, dramatic) and latent-reasoning architectures (COCONUT works, but isn't deployed at frontier) — not on observed drift.
  • Reliable horizons lag headline ones. 80%-success horizons run ~10× below the 50% headline for recent Claude models (Opus 4.6: 70 min vs 12 h). Indexing L to reliable autonomy drops today's levels ~10×, though the growth rates change less.
  • Mind the normalization. Per hour of AI work (rather than per task), the burden grows only ~4×/yr — the H term cancels. Per-task is the right frame for "grok what the agents did on this task," but quote the right number for the right claim.
  • The A trend is assumed. Agents-per-task over time is genuinely unmeasured in public. It is the weakest link in the 32×/yr figure.

Discussion

The BOTEC supports the qualitative conclusion with a sharper structure. A and L constitute a throughput problem that AI monitors genuinely solve. The floor under the whole strategy is N·U: monitors can read everything and still miss what never reaches tokens — a factor that already sits at a ~2% catch rate for silent hacks in the worst measured settings, and doubles yearly on the cleanest, least-contested trend in the set.

Monitoring's bandwidth problem is solvable and its visibility problem is not — and the visibility problem has a measured doubling time.

Follow-ups worth running

  • Measure g directly. Human time to verify (not read) one agent action as a function of the hidden computation behind it. Nobody has this curve; it is the load-bearing free parameter here.
  • A-trend telemetry. Agents-per-task over time from our own arch2/concierge fleet logs — a dataset that doesn't exist in public.
  • Re-run the OpenAI obfuscation experiment at 2026 scale as a U-trend measurement: the 2025-26 monitorability papers only cover models without CoT pressure.
