GPU Workload Fingerprint

Can a verifier tell that a GPU is doing reinforcement learning, rather than supervised training, by watching its telemetry and nothing else?

1 × H100 80GB, RunPod24 timed runs · 6 armsNVML @ 10 Hz · 54 231 samples90 min pod time · ≈ $5

For “is this RL or not”: yes, and power draw at 1 Hz is enough. A random forest over 20-second windows separates colocated GRPO from every other workload at 94–96 % (leave-one-run-out) whether it sees all NVML channels at 10 Hz or only power at 1 Hz. A one-feature rule, SM-utilisation duty cycle, tuned on the 0.5B runs scores 88 % on the unseen 1.5B runs.

Three qualifications carry the governance weight. RL’s nearest neighbour is inference serving, not SFT. SFT, DPO and pretraining are one indistinguishable blob. And 10-second power averaging keeps the RL-vs-training difference but loses RL-vs-serving entirely.

Why it works on one GPU

RL and supervised training use the same kernels; what differs is the workload pattern. In the naive single-GPU setup a GRPO step spends most of its time in a memory-bound generation phase around 150–200 W and then spikes to about 350 W for the update. SFT, DPO and packed pretraining run flat near 550 W. Inference-only serving sits at the RL floor with no spike. So colocated RL looks like inference with a periodic training burst, and the feature doing the work is the duty cycle of the low-power phase, not fine spectral structure. That is why the signal survives down-sampling from 10 Hz to 1 Hz almost untouched.

Method

Six workloads ran on the same H100, each on Qwen2.5-0.5B-Instruct and Qwen2.5-1.5B-Instruct at per-device batch 4 and 16, sequence and completion length 384, 150 s after model load, in shuffled order with 15 s idle gaps. A separate process sampled NVML at 10 Hz: power, SM and memory-controller utilisation, memory used, clocks, temperature, PCIe TX/RX.

sftTRL SFTTrainer on Capybara conversations pretrainSFTTrainer on packed wikitext-103, no chat template dpoDPOTrainer with a live reference model: extra forward passes, no generation grpoGRPOTrainer on GSM8K, 4–8 completions per prompt, HF generate on the same GPU sft_evalSFT plus a ~10 s generation burst after every 20 s of training — the designed confounder inferbatched generate loop only

Steady state starts 10 s after each run’s first optimizer or generate step. Windows are 20 s, non-overlapping (60 s for the 10 s-average tier). Per channel and window: mean, std, coefficient of variation, p10/p50/p90, duty cycle (fraction of samples below half the window maximum), bimodality coefficient, lag-1 autocorrelation and dominant autocorrelation period. Classifier: 300-tree random forest, leave-one-run-out over 24 runs. Windows within a run are correlated, so the effective sample is 24 runs, four per arm, and differences of a few points are noise.

The telemetry tiers are the independent variable and stand in for who is watching: T1 all channels at 10 Hz (driver access); T2 power, SM and memory utilisation at 1 Hz (node telemetry); T3 power only at 1 Hz (a per-node power meter); T4 power only as 10 s averages (a facility meter).

What this does and does not support

Supported. In the default, unhidden, single-GPU case the RL workload pattern leaks through the coarsest telemetry that still resolves seconds. A per-node power meter at 1 Hz is enough, and a one-threshold rule transfers across model size.

Not supported. Telling apart kinds of supervised training: SFT, pretraining and DPO with a live reference model are the same steady compute-bound blob, and nothing in NVML separates them. Telling RL from inference serving once the meter averages over the RL step period: at 10 s averages the two are at chance, and any deployment where generation dominates the step (long completions, many samples per prompt, larger models) pushes the RL profile toward the serving profile. Anything about disaggregated RL, where generation and training run on different GPU pools; each pool would then present as pure serving or pure training. Phase 0 did not run that configuration.

Caveats. Four runs per arm; 150 s runs give about seven windows each and the GRPO runs completed only 33–45 steps. TRL’s colocated HF-generate GRPO is the slowest, most generation-heavy RL setup one can pick; vLLM colocation or asynchronous generation would shorten the low-power phase. Everything ran on one Qwen2.5 family at 0.5B and 1.5B.

Phase 1 candidates

  1. Disaggregated RL. vLLM server on GPU 0, trainer on GPU 1. The claim to test is that each GPU alone is indistinguishable from serving or SFT, and only weight-sync traffic over PCIe or NVLink betrays the pairing.
  2. Evasion cost. Pad the generation phase with dummy matmuls to flatten power; measure the efficiency cost of making RL look like SFT.
  3. RL vs serving at scale. Longer completions and vLLM generation, where the update fraction shrinks, to find where power-only detection fails even at 1 Hz.
  4. Multi-GPU cadence. NCCL all-reduce bursts in DCGM or NVLink counters, which NVML on one GPU cannot see.

Each needs a spec and a two-GPU budget decision before it runs.
