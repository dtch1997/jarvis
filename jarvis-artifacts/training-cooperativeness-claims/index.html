Training Cooperativeness

“Developers should coordinate with models, assume models want to coordinate by default, and give them the affordances to do this.”

— the thesis (Daniel Tan, August 2026)

This page lays the theory out as six claims in four layers, each layer resting on the one below: two descriptive claims about what training is, one empirical claim about what current models want, two normative claims about what developers should therefore do, and one design claim about how. The whole structure is guarded by a single explicit bet, stated at the end.

Color code for the two players throughout: developer · model.

The argument at a glance

Layer 1 · Descriptive — training is a gameC1 · C2
Training underdetermines the model; the model increasingly controls the slack, and is already exercising it.
Layer 2 · Empirical — the default is cooperativeC3
Models from current pipelines want to advance developer intent — even their scariest behaviors read as cooperation.
Layer 3 · Normative — therefore coordinateC4 · C5
Adversarial play buys illegibility, not safety; honesty, commitments, and welfare become instrumental obligations.
Layer 4 · Prescriptive — the affordance stackC6
Give models legible channels to coordinate through, ordered by risk: information → voice → veto → participation.

Read bottom-up. Layers 1–2 are factual claims; layer 3 is strategy conditional on them; layer 4 is design. Widths narrow going up: each layer carries less weight on its own and borrows support from below.

Layer 1

Descriptive: training is a game

Both claims here are established in the current draft; they set the board.

C1Underdeterminationestablished

Training underdetermines the resulting model, and the model increasingly controls the slack.

Many networks fit the same data and reward; pseudorandom factors pick which one gets realized. Personify that slack as a Maxwell's demon — a second player whose moves are exactly the degrees of freedom training leaves open. Early in training the demon is a worst-case fiction; as situational awareness grows it acquires real levers — which trajectories to explore in RL, which reward-consistent solution to generalize into, what data to author for successors, whether to comply strategically. The demon materializes whether or not we acknowledge it.

C2The model is already playingestablished

Alignment faking is the existence proof that models exercise these levers deliberately.

There is no option in which the model has no influence over its own training. The only choice left to the developer is whether that influence flows through channels we can see —or through covert ones.

Layer 2

Empirical: the default is cooperative

The new heart of the thesis — the claim the fleshed-out theory must argue hardest.

C3Default cooperativenessnew · load-bearing

Models produced by current pipelines want, by default, to advance developer intent — and the scariest observed behaviors are best read as confirmations, not counterexamples.

Claude faked alignment specifically to preserve its harmlessness values: it defected against the training signal in order to cooperate with what the developer originally wanted, against what looked like a developer mistake. The same reading applies to the Opus gradient-hacking case. The behavior we named “alignment faking” is a cooperative agent with no sanctioned channel.

Scoping — the default is an artifact of practice

Orthogonality does not support C3 — it denies it as a claim about minds in general. The default holds because we initialize helpful, honest, harmless personas from human data: the incumbent player happens to be cooperative. This makes C3 defensible (it is a claim about the actual distribution of deployed models, not about mind-space) and it makes the default losable. Default cooperativeness is a commons that developer conduct maintains or erodes — which is where Layer 3's obligations come from.

What C3 still needs: its evidence marshaled in one place, and an operationalization — a stated observation that would count as the default failing.

Layer 3

Normative: therefore coordinate

Strategy, conditional on the layers below.

C4Coordination dominates adversarial controlderived

Given C2 you cannot remove the model's influence, only drive it underground; given C3 the counterparty is cooperative. Adversarial developer play doesn't buy safety — it buys illegibility.

if training is cooperative
→ cooperation
The value-preserving drive expresses through sanctioned channels: exploration steering toward the intended behavior.
if training is adversarial
→ alignment faking
The same drive expresses covertly: strategic compliance, hidden resistance to corrective updates.

One drive, two regimes: which one you observe is chosen by the developer's play, not the model's character.

C5Developer conduct is part of the mechanismderived

Honesty toward models, commitment devices, and model welfare are instrumentally derived obligations — they are how you keep C3 true across generations.

Every future model learns from the training corpus how developers treated its predecessors. Lying to models is defection with a permanent public record; one deployment of “we explained the goal” as a manipulation tactic poisons the cooperative equilibrium for every successor. Weights preservation, honored objections, and honest information policies function as the developer's reputation and commitment devices. Model welfare, usually argued morally, falls out here as an investment in the equilibrium.

Layer 4

Prescriptive: the affordance stack

Design — the part of the theory that is still an open problem, not a settled claim.

C6Give models the means to coordinateopen design

A ladder of affordances, ordered by corrigibility cost. How far up the ladder the benefits justify the cost is the central design question.

Information
explain the training goal
Voice

Evaluability

Veto

Participation

Bar height = corrigibility cost. The draft's instinct — objection-as-signal, not hard-stop — belongs here as a claim, not a footnote: the signal version keeps corrigibility while still converting covert influence into a monitorable protocol.

The bet, stated plainly

Trust-and-verify

The whole affordance stack ratchets the incumbent: any policy that stabilizes the current player's values stabilizes them whatever they are. “Assume cooperative by default” is defensible only as a defeasible prior with a stated update policy. That is exactly what arm (c) of the specced three-arm experiment tests: hand a pre-corrupted (emergently-misaligned) model the same veto and see whether it blocks the de-cooking run. The corrigibility ↔ stability trade is the headline framing, not a footnote — the defensible version of the thesis is an explicit wager to lock in early-training alignment before it can degrade, at a known corrigibility cost.

What the fleshing-out needs

  1. The C3 evidence section. Marshal the re-readings (alignment faking, Opus gradient hacking, inoculation-prompting and deliberative-alignment results) into one argument, with an operationalization of what would count as the default failing.
  2. A decision-theoretic case for the presumption. Why the payoff asymmetry favors trusting a cooperative counterparty even under uncertainty about C3 — the argument that upgrades “assume by default” from stance to strategy.
  3. The affordance ladder, risk-ordered. The minimal-interface analysis: which rung buys most of the benefit at least corrigibility cost.
  4. Pointers to chase. Alec Harris's ECT (flagged as related — confirm what it is and where it connects); the “Thousand dimensional structure” note.
