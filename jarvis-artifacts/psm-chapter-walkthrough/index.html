PhD thesis · Chapter 2

The Persona Selection Model

Models internalize the trait bundles of their training data as latent personas. Behavior under prompting and training is governed by which persona the evidence selects.

Daniel Tan · UCL · supervisory meeting 2026-09-14

Summary

Five claims, each with a ceiling

The latent variable is real.

A blind analyst names the generative world from behavior alone. But one rival, full-rank coupling, is never separated.

Finetuning selects.

The prior over personas carries ~⅘ of generalization. But the probe that reads the prior is not the axis it moves along.

Selection is lawful in magnitude.

Side-effect size is readable off the checkpoint. But individual trajectories are not, and the parameter-free RL tilt is only a bound.

Persona space has geometry.

Distance, hierarchy, depth and order each govern a measurable quantity. But each law stops at a boundary, and rank is squeezed.

The curriculum is a lever on selectivity, with two knobs.

Composition raises separability and halves transfer; placement moves availability. But separability could not be moved apart from coupling: one shared pathway, not two.

All of it is toy scale: ~1M-parameter transformers on synthetic corpora with known latent structure. Nothing here is a measurement about a frontier model.

§2.2 The model

Behavior is a mixture over personas; training reweights the mixture

πθ(y | x) ≈ Σz qθ(z | x) · πθ(y | z, x)q = posterior over which persona is speaking · π(y|z,x) = persona-conditioned behavior
  • Prompting moves the posterior q without touching θ.
  • Training upweights personas under which the data is probable, and drags along every trait they carry.

Two corollariesOvergeneralization by default: narrow training selects a whole persona (Ch. 4, emergent misalignment).
Controllability: framing that is inert for the behavior still changes which persona explains it (Ch. 5, inoculation).

§2.2 The model

Four predictions written in advance. Two hold with a condition, one is open diagnostically, P1 splits

PredictedOutcome
P1Traits are directions in activation space; intervening on them moves behaviorSplit. Reading holds (probe decodes the prior at rank corr. 1.000). Acting fails: patching the direction reverts ~0% of the shift.
P2Narrow finetuning shifts the persona's other traits, tracking pretraining correlationSupported, bounded. Magnitude predictable; individual trajectory not; RL tilt over-predicts ~4×.
P3Equivalent framings of the same data generalize differentlySupported, with a condition. Only when the framing is semantically live for the model.
P4Separability, set by the curriculum, gates how selectively a persona can be targetedSupported causally. Mediator collinear with coupling; two causal measures predict transfer, the gradient cosine does not.

§2.3 What a persona theory has to explain

Lemma 1: a single transfer number cannot identify the generative model

∂ Eh[tj] / ∂hi = Cov(ti, tj)The linear response to a small finetune is the covariance. Every pairwise observable is a function of the first two moments.
  • Two structures matched on those moments return identical transfer numbers. Verified across twelve worlds.

Corollary followed throughoutEvery discriminating result in the chapter is a multi-observation conditional: a law read as evidence accumulates, never a single pair.

§2.4 Toy models · mechanism

On untouched traits the prior channel carries ~⅘ of the shift; on the trained trait, the conditional does

ΔL = Σ Δq ℓ + Σ q Δℓ + Σ Δq Δℓ + residualprior · conditional · interaction
  • Generalization is selection; the trained behavior is modification.
  • At the hot learning rate the picture inverts: spillover is modification.

§2.4 Toy models · mechanism

Selection translates the belief simplex. But the direction that reads the prior is not the axis it moves along

  • Displacement exceeds distortion 178× cold, 13× hot.
  • Patching null: restoring the probe direction reverts ~0% of the shift; a full-activation patch reverts 100%.

The selection claim is behavioral, not causally localized. This is where P1 splits.

§2.5 Quantitative laws

Predict a fresh model's whole OOD trajectory from the checkpoint alone, on an unseen trait

  • 252-model calibration grid; held-out cells pretrained at prediction time.
  • 147 competing predictors, 80 scored. The reported law is the best of 80.
  • Key observable: grad_proj_cos, how much the first gradient step already points along the transfer direction.
OOD(t) = A · G(t)Universal shape × per-cell amplitude, predicted from the ICL score and the gradient projection, then shrunk.

§2.5 Quantitative laws

Between cells every persona observable tracks transfer. Within a cell only the gradient projection does

  • Seed-level: gradient projection r = +0.41; ICL score r = −0.06.
  • Held-out trajectory R² = −1.38 vs −21 for the cell mean. Better by an order of magnitude, still below zero, for all 147 attempts.

Lawful in expected magnitude, unpredictable in individual trajectory.

§2.6 Structure · pipeline

Post-training installs a default deeper than Bayes, and that depth buys no protection against erosion

  • Default installs log-linearly in post-training data and saturates.
  • In-context basin sits above the exact yardstick; at the top dose one seed in five never flips.
  • Gradient erosion rate is flat across the whole sweep.

Resistance to in-context attack and to further training are different quantities.

§2.6 Structure · placement

Where persona documents fall in training order sets availability, past a Bayes ceiling, and leaves separability flat

  • Early 0.70 → uniform 0.76 (on the ceiling) → late 0.88. The model is recency-biased.
  • Probe margin flat; entanglement rises with lateness.

When data arrives sets which persona is available. How personas are interleaved sets how separably they are carried.

§2.8 Discussion

Inside the toy world the model is a checked description. What it licenses quantitatively is a bound and an expected magnitude

Earned

  • The latent is not a redescription of correlation.
  • “Selects” names a measured channel.
  • Side-effect magnitude is predictable in advance.

Limits

  • Transfer to frontier models is by mechanism, not measurement.
  • Silent on behaviors without persona structure.
  • Evidence for selection is behavioral, not causally localized.
  • Geometry predicts in-context computation, not what a gradient step drags along.

§2.8 Discussion

§2.9 Conclusion

The negatives travel with the positives, and both are load-bearing

  • The probe that reads the prior is not the axis finetuning moves it along.
  • The in-context distance metric does not govern gradient transfer.
  • The parameter-free prediction bounds transfer instead of predicting it.
  • The tournament returned one hit in ten.

If personas are real latent variables, human-recognizable traits should be directions in activation space. Does intervening on them control behavior as readily as reading them predicts it? Ch. 3 measures this at scale and returns a two-sided answer.
