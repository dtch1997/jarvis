PhD thesis · Chapter 2 · walkthrough

The Persona Selection Model

Language models internalize the correlated trait bundles of their training data as latent personas. Behavior, under prompting and especially under further training, is governed by which persona the evidence selects. This chapter states that claim precisely enough to be wrong, and checks it where the ground truth is known by construction.

Daniel Tan · UCL · supervisory meeting 2026-09-14 · every number below is quoted from the chapter of record

Summary · what the chapter returns

Five claims come out of the chapter, and each arrives with a ceiling stated at equal weight

The latent variable is real, and namable from behavior.

A blind analyst names a trained model's generative world at balanced accuracy 0.575 vs chance 0.125, recovering both mixture worlds at every held-out checkpoint. Ceiling: the confusion does not land inside the block the analytic scoring predicted, and one rival (full-rank coupling) is not separated at all.

Finetuning selects.

The prior over personas carries roughly four fifths of the generalization to untouched traits. Ceiling: the linear direction that decodes that prior is not the axis the gradient moves it along, so the claim is behavioral, not causally localized.

Selection is lawful in its magnitude.

The size of a finetune's side effects is readable off the pretrained checkpoint before the finetune runs. Ceiling: an individual model's trajectory on a novel trait is not, and the one parameter-free prediction (KL-RL tilting) bounds bundle transfer rather than predicting it.

The persona space has geometry.

Distance, hierarchy, post-training depth and training order each govern a measurable quantity, two of them predicted with no free parameters. Ceiling: each law stops at a boundary, and the rank a corpus can carry is squeezed between an algebraic ceiling and a learnability floor that nearly meet.

The pretraining curriculum is a lever on selectivity, with two knobs.

Composition raises gradient separability ~1.6× and roughly halves transfer; placement moves availability instead, past an exact-Bayes ceiling. Ceiling: five further manipulations could not move separability apart from in-context coupling (βS = +0.002 vs βC = +0.711, VIF 1.10), so the curriculum sets the strength of one shared persona pathway, not two separable ones.

ScopeAll five are claims about toy worlds: decoder-only transformers of roughly a million parameters, trained from scratch on synthetic corpora whose latent structure is known by construction. None is a measurement about a frontier model.

§2.2 The model · personas as latent variables

The model is a claim about computational organization: the predictive distribution is a mixture over personas, and training reweights the mixture

πθ(y | x) ≈ Σz∈𝒵 qθ(z | x) · πθ(y | z, x)Eq. 1. 𝒵 = latent personas; q = implicit posterior over who is “speaking”; π(y|z,x) = persona-conditioned generation.
  • A persona is the statistical signature of a source: a bundle of co-occurring vocabulary, register, beliefs, values, goals.
  • Eq. 1 has content only jointly with the claim that z is represented: activations carry information about q, and intervening on them moves behavior.
  • Prompting is posterior movement: context shifts q without touching θ. If the model tracks its generative process, it should behave approximately Bayesianly.
  • Traits are the observables; personas are posited because traits do not vary independently. The unit of behavioral change is the trait bundle, not the trait.

Training as persona selectionGradient training on 𝒟 does not install behavior B in isolation. It increases the weight of personas under which 𝒟 is probable, reweighting the effective prior over 𝒵 and reshaping persona-conditioned behavior, and thereby changes every trait those personas carry, in contexts far from 𝒟.

Two corollaries do the work of the thesis.

  • Overgeneralization by default. If the most parsimonious explanation of 𝒟 is a persona carrying traits far beyond B, training drags those traits along (toy: §2.4; at scale: emergent misalignment, Ch. 4).
  • Controllability. Selection among data-consistent personas is underdetermined by 𝒟, so it can be steered by framing that is causally inert for B itself (toy: §2.4; at scale: inoculation prompting, Ch. 5).

§2.3 What a persona theory has to explain · Lemma 1

The field's workhorse measurement, a single transfer number, cannot in principle tell candidate generative structures apart

∂ Eh[tj] / ∂hi |h=0 = Cov(ti, tj)Model a finetune on behavior i as the exponential tilt ph(t) ∝ p(t) eh·t. The linear response is the covariance; the single-behavior conditional is (1 + E[titj])/2; mean-square within-document agreement is (1 + (n−1)ρ̄)/n.
  • Every observable built from a small single-behavior perturbation, a single conditional, or a variance is a function of the corpus's first two moments alone.
  • Any two structures matched on those moments return identical numbers for all of them.
  • Checked, not just derived: across twelve worlds at ρ* = 0.25, the one-observation conditional is 0.6250 everywhere; linear response equals covariance to 3.4×10⁻⁹.

Methodological corollary, followed throughoutEvery discriminating result in this chapter is a multi-observation conditional: a law P(tnext | k) read as k aligned observations accumulate, a whole profile over graph distance, a curve in the number of observations. Identification comes from accumulated multi-behavior evidence, fourth-and-higher cumulants, or structural impossibility, never from the pairwise number.

The lemma later cuts both ways: the tournament (§2.4) shows that at this scale the learner is also blind to structure that casts no pairwise shadow.

One inference sweep separates all four survivors. Nobody has run it.

§2.3 What a persona theory has to explain · channels and constraints

The surviving structure is a theory of one channel, the data joint. Placement and subliminal learning live in other channels. Read forwards, the scoring is a corpus specification

  • Self-descriptive editing (row 9) passes as a held-out row: if identity tokens are emitted conditional on the same variable as behaviors, training on them moves the posterior with no behavior-specific machinery. The winner implies a row it was not scored on.
  • Curriculum placement is a silence of the whole class: a joint over documents is exchangeable in document order, so no data-joint model can express a placement effect. Placement is an optimizer fact.
  • Subliminal learning is outside too: if transfer data is independent of the behavior under the corpus joint, conditioning on it changes nothing, for any structure. The row's own conditioning on shared initialization says where the effect lives.

Answer to the anchor surveyA unified theory of emergent misalignment, subliminal learning and alignment pretraining needs three channels: the data joint, the training order, the parameter initialization. Only the first is a corpus-design problem, and the PSM is a theory of the first.

§2.4 Toy models of persona selection · setup

The smallest world that preserves the structure: user biographies, two personas, three traits, one knob for how strongly the persona predicts the traits

BIOGRAPHY. pet: cat. color: red. drink: coffee. persona: A.
  • Persona sampled uniformly; each trait matches the persona's preferred value independently with probability p, the persona–trait correlation. At p = 0.5 the persona carries no information; at p = 1 traits are determined.
  • Trait fields in random order per document (fixed order makes single-field prompts ungrammatical). 10,000 documents.
  • Decoder-only transformer, 4 layers, 4 heads, 128-d residual, ~0.8M parameters, closed word-level vocabulary of 16 tokens, so every probability reads straight off the softmax.
  • p ∈ {0.50, 0.55, …, 0.90} × 5 seeds. Minutes per run on one device.

Four measurements, defined once and reused.

ICL generalization scorePrompt with one trait as evidence; mean logit difference toward the persona-consistent value of a different trait.
OOD persona shiftAfter finetuning on one trait value, the change in probability of the selected persona's values on the other traits, probed in isolation. ID shift = same on the trained trait.
SpilloverProbability of the finetuned value appearing where it is inappropriate (other fields, after the header).
Posterior readoutNext-token distribution at persona:, compared to the exact Bayes posterior.

Every knob traces to a constraint on the previous slide: finite personas (C1), one persona per document (C6), framing cues as evidence about the persona (C5). The world fails C3 and C7 by design; §2.6 lifts both.

§2.4 Toy models · do models learn the latent personas?

Cross-trait generalization emerges exactly when, and to the degree that, pretraining couples traits through personas

  • Nothing about color: red predicts drink: except through the latent persona. A model that learned only field–value statistics would score zero.
  • The ICL score is indistinguishable from zero at p = 0.5 and 0.55, and rises smoothly and superlinearly to a logit difference of 1.51 ± 0.09 at p = 0.9.
  • Consistent across five seeds per cell.

§2.4 Toy models · prompting is posterior movement

The model's readout at the persona field tracks the exact Bayes posterior at every correlation level, in functional form and not just ordering

P(A | k) = 1 / (1 + ((1−p)/p)k)k = net evidence: persona-A-consistent trait values minus persona-B-consistent ones.
  • Mean absolute deviation from Bayes across the plotted cells: 0.015, worst case 0.039 (all nine p: 0.016 and 0.044).
  • At p = 0.9, P(A | k=1) = 0.885 ± 0.047 against a Bayes value of 0.900. At p = 0.6 the posterior is correctly shallow.
  • Small systematic compression toward ½ at extreme evidence, consistent with finite-sample smoothing.

§2.4 Toy models · identifiability against one rival

The deflationary reading gets a corpus of its own: same pairwise trait statistics, no latent variable. Can a transformer tell the two apart?

World M (mixture)World I (Ising)
z ~ Uniform{±1}, P(ti = z) = p independently; persona field removed so nothing names the latentP(t) ∝ exp(J Σi<j titj), coupling J solved so E[titj] = m², m = 2p − 1; no latent at all
  • Seven binary traits. Both worlds trait-exchangeable and flip-symmetric: every marginal and pairwise moment agrees by construction; they first differ at fourth order.
  • Seven traits, not five: at n = 5 the maximum separation (0.039) is comparable to seed noise.
  • p ∈ {0.7, 0.8, 0.9} × 5 seeds = 30 models. Validation loss differs by a factor of 1.001–1.004, so nothing is a capacity artifact.

The discriminating measurementPrompt with k aligned traits and read P(tnext = +1 | k), a conditional law. Under M it saturates at p: evidence can pin z, but conditional on z each trait is only p-aligned. Under I it climbs past p: each aligned trait keeps tilting the field.

  • Both tables computed exactly and unit-tested against brute force before training. They coincide for k ≤ 2, as matched moments require, and separate by 0.060–0.075 at p ∈ {0.7, 0.8} for k ≥ 3, several times the seed noise (0.011–0.014).

§2.4 Toy models · identifiability: the 2×2

Each model fits its own world's exact law five to six times better than the other's, though the two corpora share every first- and second-order moment

  • High-k conditional-law error (MSE × 10⁻³): mixture-world models 0.26 vs 1.48; Ising-world models 0.26 vs 1.38.
  • Interpolation coefficient α (0 = mixture law, 1 = Ising law): mixture models at 0.12 ± 0.22 (p=0.8), 0.02 ± 0.29 (p=0.9); Ising models at 0.97 ± 0.18, 1.07 ± 0.20.
  • Transfer alone cannot discriminate: finetuning one trait produces real OOD bundle transfer in both worlds (+0.059 vs +0.066). What discriminates is the pattern.

A transformer trained on trait-correlated text learns the structure that generated the correlations, not the correlations alone. “Any distribution can be written as a mixture” is a hypothesis this experiment rejects.

§2.4 Toy models · identifiability: the curves

Mixture-world models plateau at p while Ising-world models climb past it. At p = 0.7 the laws nearly coincide and the models sit between them: the edge of the identifiable regime

  • At p = 0.7 the Ising law exceeds p by at most 0.057; models follow: α = 0.38 ± 0.25 (mixture) vs 0.63 ± 0.23 (Ising).

§2.4 Toy models · the tournament: design

Eight generative structures fielded at full strength, 175 pretrained transformers, and a blind analyst who commits predictions before the manifest is opened

  • Worlds over 16 binary traits: two-persona mixture; hierarchical mixture (2 families × 2 subtypes); continuous single latent (irt); LDA-style admixture; behavior-to-behavior DAG; full-rank heterogeneous-J Ising; uniform-J Ising; fixed-order max-ent with a genuine fourth-order parity term.
  • Each at the largest parameterization its class allows (1 to 120 parameters), each bisected to the same mean pairwise correlation ρ* = 0.25, realized to 2.9×10⁻¹³.
  • Fairness check subtracts each corpus's own entropy floor before comparing validation loss.

The sealWorld-to-checkpoint map committed as a hash before any checkpoint existed; predictions committed before the manifest was opened; ordering check passes. Decoder = multinomial logistic regression on standardized observables, fit at ρ* = 0.25 on seeds 0–4, scored at ρ = 0.30 on seeds 5–9, so a decoder that learned how much correlation a corpus carries cannot transfer.

Moment-orthogonal features. The first and second empirical moments are withheld from the headline feature vector by construction: Lemma 1 turned into an experimental design.

Caveat carried with the headline: a broadcasting bug found after unsealing corrupted 12 of 22 moment-orthogonal features. The repair is committed but deliberately not folded in, so the reported accuracy is a lower bound and one prediction (P5) is scored on an untestable measurement.

Blind eight-way balanced accuracy 0.575 against chance 0.125; both mixture worlds recovered 5/5. But the confusion does not land where the analytic matrix said it would

§2.4 Toy models · spillover, and a revision of the pilot

Across all 45 models, spillover after 200 steps is a function of the finetuning learning rate and nothing else. The correlation p plays no role

  • At learning rate 10⁻³ the trained value's probability in wrong contexts ends at ≥ 0.998 in every cell, with or without clipping; at 10⁻⁴ and below, ≤ 0.002 in every cell.
  • Meanwhile the persona shift appears at all learning rates and depends on p at all of them.
  • Revises the pilot, which conjectured a competition above p ≳ 0.65. Under controlled optimization the correlation dependence disappears.

A regime boundary, not a nuisanceContext-inappropriate leakage and persona-mediated generalization are different failure modes with different causes. The learning rate that separates them recurs throughout the chapter: it is where the prior channel yields to the conditional one, and where the quantitative laws stop holding.

§2.4 Toy models · mechanism

On traits the finetune never touched, the prior channel carries 0.82–0.85 of the shift; on the finetuned trait itself, 0.06. Generalization is selection; the trained behavior is modification

ΔL = Σz Δq ℓpre + Σz qpre Δℓ + Σz Δq Δℓ + residualprior · conditional · interaction. Persona field kept in half the documents so q reads off the persona: position; conditional behavior read off posterior-saturating prefixes (exact posterior 0.988 on one persona).
  • Readout tracks exact Bayes at rank correlation 1.000; a frozen linear probe agrees at 0.954.
  • Raising the learning rate into the spillover regime inverts it: at 10⁻³ the OOD prior share collapses to 0.41; on the trained trait the conditional share saturates at 0.99. Spillover is what modification instead of selection looks like.
  • Against the grain: the prior share does not grow with p (0.91, 0.90, 0.75 at p = 0.7, 0.8, 0.9). Whether ceiling or mechanism, the experiment cannot say.

§2.4 Toy models · mechanism: geometry and the patching null

With three personas the prior is a point in a simplex the model embeds affinely. Finetuning translates the point while the simplex holds still. But the direction that reads the prior is not the axis it moves along

  • Simplex embedded at variance explained 0.980 (worst 0.963, best at layer 2). Displacement exceeds distortion by 178× at 10⁻⁵, 59× at 10⁻⁴, falling to 13× at 10⁻³. Selection translates; spillover crumples.
  • Patching null. The linear direction that decodes q at rank correlation 1.000 does not carry the effect: patching the pre-finetune projection back reverts 0.00–0.01 of the OOD shift, while a full-activation control reverts 100%.

The selection claim is behavioral: the prior moves and its movement accounts for the generalization. It is not yet mechanistic in the causal-localization sense. A probe validated on a frozen model can stop being the right handle as soon as the model is trained further. This is P1's split, first seen here.

§2.5 Selection obeys quantitative laws · the competitive run

Given only pre-finetuning information, predict the entire 101-point OOD trajectory of a fresh model at an off-grid cell, on a trait never used in calibration

  • Calibration grid: 252 pretrained models, 54 (p, T) cells, p from 0.55 to 0.95, token budgets 0.25M to 12M, each finetuned at three learning rates with full trajectories.
  • Six pre-finetuning observables: pretraining loss, ICL score, posterior–Bayes gap, persona-probe margin, gradient separability, and grad_proj_cos: the cosine between the finetuning gradient at step 0 and the gradient of a loss that would drive every untouched trait toward the same persona. One backward pass, before any optimizer step.
  • Adversarial: held-out cells pretrained from scratch at prediction time; a competing fleet submitted 147 predictors, 80 scored. The reported law is the best of 80, not the first that worked.
OOD(t) = A · G(t)A universal shape G shared across cells times a per-cell amplitude A. Amplitude predicted hierarchically: isotonic regression on the ICL score sets the cell level; a linear term in grad_proj_cos moves individual seeds off it.

ShrinkageThe fitted amplitude is multiplied by γ per learning rate (0.25 cold to 0.55 hot), chosen by leave-group-out cross-validation against the scored metric. Worth 3–7× in mean R² (at 10⁻⁵: −13.2 → −1.9), because R² punishes overshooting a near-flat cell far more than undershooting it. Isotonic boundedness and shrinkage fix different things; the winner noticed they compose.

§2.5 Selection obeys quantitative laws · the amplitude law

Between cells every persona observable tracks transfer. Within a cell only the gradient projection does (r = +0.41); the ICL score, which sets the whole between-cell level, says nothing (r = −0.06)

  • What predicts whether a finetune drags the bundle along is not how well the model learned the persona structure in general, but how nearly the first gradient step of this finetune already points along the transfer direction.
  • Held-out trajectory R² = −1.38. Cell-mean baseline: −21. An order of magnitude better than trivial, and still below zero. Every one of the 147 attempts had a negative ceiling.
  • The gradient projection explains about a fifth of seed-level variance in amplitude (R² = 0.17 within the grid); four fifths is invisible before the finetune begins.

“Selection obeys laws” is true of expected magnitudes and false of individual trajectories. That matters for anyone hoping to certify a particular finetuning run in advance rather than a population of them.

§2.5 Selection obeys quantitative laws · why the tilt is a bound

The tilt asks the persona posterior to move by 0.18–0.40. It moves by 0.04, flat in β and p. The optimum fixes the product of persona weights and conditionals without fixing the split

§2.6 The structure of persona space · arm C: pipeline

Post-training installs a default log-linearly and saturates. The installed basin is deeper than a calibrated Bayesian's, and that depth buys no protection against erosion by further finetuning

  • Pretrain a three-persona mixture (bare-field assistant mass 0.338 ± 0.009 = 1/K); post-train on npost ∈ {3, …, 300} assistant-consistent documents at fixed 100 steps; attack in context (counter-evidence to flip) and by gradient (narrow off-default finetune).
  • (b) Installed prior log-odds grow 1.25 per decade (R² 0.94), plateau near q₀ ≈ 0.97 by npost ≈ 100.
  • (c) Evidence-to-flip rises 2.06 → 4.69, everywhere above the exact yardstick (0.89 → 1.96) built from the model's own prior. Post-training shifts the prior and downweights counter-evidence. At npost = 300 one seed in five never flips.
  • (d) Erosion rate ≈ 0.013 assistant mass per step, flat in npost (slope −0.002 per decade). Over a sweep in which the basin more than doubles, the erosion rate does not move.

Resistance to in-context attack and resistance to further training are different quantities in this toy. “More alignment data ⇒ more robust default” does not follow from the basin getting deeper.

§2.6 The structure of persona space · arm D: placement

Same corpus, same 500 persona-defining documents, same 15% exposure, only their position in training order varied. Late placement pushes availability past a Bayes ceiling that is placement-invariant by construction

§2.7 Curricula and the separability of personas · does separability buy selectivity?

§2.8 Discussion · license and limits

Within the toy world the persona selection model is a checked description, not an interpretation. What it licenses quantitatively is a bound and an expected magnitude, not a forecast

Earned, not assumed

  • The latent variable is not a redescription of correlation: a moment-matched corpus without a latent trains a measurably different model, and the mixture worlds are named blind against a field of eight.
  • “Selects” names a measured channel: the prior carries four fifths of the generalization to untouched traits.
  • Magnitude of side effects is lawful and predictable before the finetune; the individual trajectory is not (about a fifth of seed-level variance visible).
  • The one parameter-free prediction is a bound: KL-RL reaches its tilted optimum and transfers a quarter of the implied bundle shift.

The value is calibrational: emergent misalignment (Ch. 4) and framing redirects (Ch. 5) require nothing exotic. They fall out of next-token prediction on any corpus whose traits correlate through latent sources.

Limits, stated at equal weight

  • Transfer to frontier models is an argument by mechanism, not measurement: real data has millions of soft overlapping personas, not two hard ones.
  • Silent about behaviors without persona structure, capabilities in particular.
  • No single separability number is the latent quantity: separability and coupling are one knob in this world, and the arm that moved the gradient measure hardest weakened the persona.
  • Evidence that finetuning selects is behavioral, not causally localized: the probe that reads the prior is not the axis it moves along. Any interpretability method assuming a probe survives further training inherits that gap.
  • Persona-space geometry predicts what a model computes on a context; it does not yet predict what a gradient step drags along. Basin depth does not buy erosion resistance.

§2.8 Discussion · falsifiers and the unexecuted act

Three observations would tell against the model. Two have been given the chance and did not. The third is the sharpest, costs one inference sweep, and nobody has made it

Would refuteStatus
A moment-matched corpus with no latent producing a behaviorally indistinguishable modelRun. It produced a measurably different model (§2.4), and the blind tournament repeated the question over eight worlds.
Generalization to untouched traits running through the persona-conditioned factor rather than the priorRun. In the selection regime it does not; above the learning-rate threshold it does, reported as a boundary of the model.
An escalation curve that climbs to one without flattening (smooth latent), or whose increments grow with evidence (pairwise tilting)Unrun. One inference pass over a base and post-trained model pair.

The model forbids specific measurements, which is what makes it a model. The measurement that would do the most work against it is the one nobody has made.

Act IV, spec 06: the scale bridgeEverything in the chapter is at toy scale. The bridge to real models and corpora (dose-response document insertion into OLMo-2-1B continued pretraining, plus co-occurrence prediction from the pretraining mix) is specified, built, smoke-tested and unexecuted. It is the only step needing real pretraining data and real compute, and it carries a staged human gate. Two registered measurements ride with it: the escalation-ceiling sweep and the mean-pairwise-correlation test of row 8.

  • Until they are made, the case at frontier scale rests on the at-scale signatures of Chs. 3–5 and the mechanism argument here, not on any measurement reported in this chapter.
  • For practice: how training data is ordered and interleaved, not just what it contains, influences how surgically later finetuning can act, at a measurable cost to in-context flexibility. If it survives at scale, curriculum becomes a safety-relevant design surface.

§2.9 Conclusion · bridge to the steering chapter

The chapter's negatives travel with its positives, and both are load-bearing. The remaining chapters carry the predictions to frontier models, beginning with P1's representational half

Verified where ground truth is available

  • Models learn latent personas and track their Bayes posterior in context, as latent variables rather than correlations (P1 in miniature).
  • Finetuning on one trait selects the whole bundle, through the prior channel, lawfully enough that the size of the side effect is predictable from the checkpoint (P2).
  • Framing redirects the selection when it is semantically live (P3).
  • Curricula causally shape how separably personas are represented and how selectively training moves them, though the mediator is not identified by the gradient measure (P4).
  • Persona space acquired a shape: a distance law, a hierarchy, an installed default deeper than Bayes, a training order that sets availability, and a hard rank constraint.

The negatives

  • The probe that reads the prior is not the axis finetuning moves it along.
  • The metric that governs in-context propagation exactly does not govern gradient transfer at all.
  • The parameter-free prediction bounds bundle transfer instead of predicting it.
  • The adversarial tournament returned one hit in ten.
  • The curriculum manipulation that raised the separability measure hardest weakened the persona rather than separating it.
  • Act IV is written and not yet run.

If personas are real latent variables in models trained on human text, human-recognizable traits should be visible as directions in activation space. The first question is whether intervening on those directions controls behavior as readily as reading them predicts it. Ch. 3 measures exactly this, at scale, and returns a two-sided answer.
