PhD thesis · Chapter 2

The Persona Selection Model

Models internalize the trait bundles of their training data as latent personas. Behavior under prompting and training is governed by which persona the evidence selects.

Daniel Tan · UCL · supervisory meeting 2026-09-14

Summary

Five claims, each with a ceiling

The latent variable is real.

A blind analyst names the generative world from behavior alone. But one rival, full-rank coupling, is never separated.

Finetuning selects.

The prior over personas carries ~⅘ of generalization. But the probe that reads the prior is not the axis it moves along.

Selection is lawful in magnitude.

Side-effect size is readable off the checkpoint. But individual trajectories are not, and the parameter-free RL tilt is only a bound.

Persona space has geometry.

Distance, hierarchy, depth and order each govern a measurable quantity. But each law stops at a boundary, and rank is squeezed.

The curriculum is a lever on selectivity, with two knobs.

Composition raises separability and halves transfer; placement moves availability. But separability could not be moved apart from coupling: one shared pathway, not two.

All of it is toy scale: ~1M-parameter transformers on synthetic corpora with known latent structure. Nothing here is a measurement about a frontier model.

Part 1

A latent-variable model of personas

Where the idea comes from, what it has to explain, which statistical models are a priori plausible, and the four predictions the thesis commits to.

1.1 Why personas

An LLM is not one author. It is a simulator of the many authors who wrote its training data

  • To predict text written by many sources, the model must infer who is writing and continue as them.
  • A persona is the bundle of traits one source carries. The "assistant" is one character the simulator can play.

Andreas (2022), Language Models as Agent Models, Findings of EMNLP · janus (2022), Simulators, LessWrong · Shanahan, McDonell & Reynolds (2023), Role play with large language models, Nature 623 · Anthropic (2026), The Persona Selection Model

1.1 Why personas · in their words

The framing is not new. What is new is making it predictive

Andreas 2022 · Findings of EMNLP"When performing next word prediction given a textual context, an LM can infer and represent properties of an agent likely to have produced that context."

janus 2022 · Simulators, LessWrong"In the agentic AI ontology, there is no difference between the policy and the effective agent, but for GPT, there is."

Shanahan, McDonell & Reynolds 2023 · Nature"We can think of an LLM as a non-deterministic simulator capable of role-playing an infinity of characters … the dialogue agent maintains a superposition of simulacra that are consistent with the preceding context."

Anthropic 2026 · The Persona Selection Model"An accurate enough autocomplete engine must learn to simulate the human-like characters appearing in text—real people, fictional characters, sci-fi robots, and so forth."

All four re-describe behavior after the fact. None commits to predictions about training dynamics, and none is scored against a catalogue it did not choose. That is the gap this chapter fills.

1.3 Candidate statistical models

Nine ways behaviors could come to correlate: a hidden cause, direct couplings, or copied exemplars

  • Left: a latent variable explains the correlations. Split by whether it takes finitely many values (personas) or varies continuously.
  • Middle: no latent; behaviors pull on each other directly.
  • Right: no structure at all, just a bank of copied documents.
  • Two identities collapse the picture: low-rank couplings are factor models, and admixture becomes a mixture as its blending vanishes.

1.3 Candidate statistical models

The field's standard measurement, "train on A, how much does B move", cannot distinguish any of these

response of B to a small push on A = Cov(A, B)Lemma 1. Single-behavior transfer, single-behavior conditionals and within-document agreement are all functions of the corpus's first two moments.
  • Any two structures with the same pairwise correlations give identical transfer numbers. Verified numerically across all twelve fielded worlds.
  • What can discriminate: how a behavior responds as evidence about several other behaviors accumulates. Every discriminating result in the chapter is of that kind.

1.3 Candidate statistical models

Scored on the regularities, four structures survive. Among them, the chapter commits to an atomic prior as a modelling choice

Hierarchical mixture5survives
Multi-factor5survives
Admixture5survives
Retrieval4survives
DAG · Ising · max-ent · IRT≤ 2eliminated

Low-rank couplings turn out to be factor models (Hubbard–Stratonovich), so the survivors differ mainly in the shape of the prior over the latent: a finite set of personas, or a continuum.

Modelling choiceReal persona structure is neither purely atomic nor purely smooth. The chapter commits to atoms, finitely many personas, because that is what makes "selection" a well-defined operation and lets every toy world carry an exact posterior.

The escalation curve (how consistently a held-out behavior follows k consistent ones) is the measurement that would locate a real model between the two limits. Registered; unrun.

1.4 The model

Behavior is a mixture over personas; training reweights the mixture

πθ(y | x) ≈ Σz qθ(z | x) · πθ(y | z, x)q = posterior over which persona is speaking · π(y|z,x) = persona-conditioned behavior
  • Prompting moves the posterior q without touching θ.
  • Training upweights personas under which the data is probable, and drags along every trait they carry.

Two corollariesOvergeneralization by default: narrow training selects a whole persona (Ch. 4, emergent misalignment).
Controllability: framing that is inert for the behavior still changes which persona explains it (Ch. 5, inoculation).

Scope: "reweights" describes the regime where pretraining dominates. Post-training can in principle create personas; the chapter does not claim otherwise.

1.4 The model

Four predictions written in advance. Two hold with a condition, one is open diagnostically, P1 splits

PredictedOutcome
P1Traits are directions in activation space; intervening on them moves behaviorSplit. Reading holds (probe decodes the prior at rank corr. 1.000). Acting fails: patching the direction reverts ~0% of the shift.
P2Narrow finetuning shifts the persona's other traits, tracking pretraining correlationSupported, bounded. Magnitude predictable; individual trajectory not; RL tilt over-predicts ~4×.
P3Equivalent framings of the same data generalize differentlySupported, with a condition. Only when the framing is semantically live for the model.
P4Separability, set by the curriculum, gates how selectively a persona can be targetedSupported causally. Mediator collinear with coupling; two causal measures predict transfer, the gradient cosine does not.

Part 1 · tl;dr

Part 1 in four lines

1An LLM simulates the many authors of its training data. A persona is one author's bundle of traits.
2Persona selection offers a compact hypothesis for ten empirical phenomena.
3Persona selection can be operationalised as a latent-variable generative model.
4The pretraining distribution determines the personas; training reweights the mixture.

CaveatLine 4 is an empirical regime, not a law. Nothing forbids post-training from adding new personas; if most compute went to post-training it plainly would. The claim holds where pretraining dominates, which is the regime studied here.

3.1 Pretraining induces the latent structure

Cross-trait generalization appears exactly as pretraining couples traits through personas

  • Show color: red, ask drink:. Nothing links them except the latent persona.
  • Zero at p = 0.5, rising to a logit difference of 1.5 at p = 0.9.

3.2 The latent variable is real

Objection: "any distribution is a mixture." Answer: build a corpus with the same pairwise statistics and no latent, and see if the model can tell

World M (mixture)World I (Ising)
Hidden persona z; each trait matches z with prob. pPairwise couplings tuned to the same trait–trait correlations; no hidden variable
  • Seven traits. Every marginal and every pairwise correlation is identical by construction.

The measurementPut k traits consistent with one persona in the prompt and read P(next trait consistent | k).
Under M: once z is pinned, each remaining trait is still only p-likely, so the curve flattens at p.
Under I: each consistent trait tilts the others a bit more, so the curve keeps rising past p.

3.2 The latent variable is real

Each model matches its own world's curve 5–6× better than the other's

  • The two corpora share every pairwise statistic. The trained models are still different, so the in-context measurement is reading structure, not correlation.
  • Finetuning transfer alone is the same in both worlds. It could not have told them apart.

Mixture models flatten at p, Ising models keep rising. At p = 0.7 the two curves nearly coincide

3.2 The latent variable is real · tournament

Train models on eight different generative worlds. Can an analyst who does not know which is which name the world from the model's behavior?

  • The eight worlds are the candidate structures of Part 1, turned into corpora over 16 traits, each at its strongest, all at the same mean correlation.
  • 175 models trained. Checkpoint labels sealed by hash before training; predictions committed before unsealing.
  • The analyst sees only behavioral observables that exclude pairwise statistics by construction.

Why it mattersIf the mixture hypothesis is right, a model trained on a mixture should look different from a model trained on any rival, to someone who does not know the answer. That is identifiability, tested honestly.

3.2 The latent variable is real · tournament

Both mixture worlds are named correctly 5/5. The failures are informative: coupling worlds blur into each other

  • Rows = true world, columns = the analyst's call. Balanced accuracy 0.575 vs chance 0.125.
  • Heterogeneous Ising is always called DAG: a trained model does not distinguish full-rank coupling from a causal graph.

Fig. 2.5

3.2 The latent variable is real · tournament

One rival world encodes an XOR rule in every document. The model never learns it

  • Parity world: some trait is the XOR of others, in 100% of documents, with pairwise correlations at zero.
  • Asked to complete the XOR, the model scores exactly 0.5 at every depth. Registered ≥ 0.85.
  • Meaning: at this scale a transformer only learns structure that shows up in pairwise statistics. A limit on the learner, and on what the tournament can decide.

Fig. 2.8

3.2 The latent variable is real · tournament

Ten pre-registered sub-predictions, one hit. The headline survives; the fine-grained predictions about where confusions land did not

  • The misses are threshold misses on details: which rivals would be confused with which, exact slopes, a control's ceiling. The accuracy bar itself was cleared.
  • The one hit (P10): a predictor built from the corpus statistics alone cannot predict transfer size. The corpus fixes the pattern of transfer; the optimizer fixes its size.

LicenseStronger than the two-world result: the mixture is identifiable blind against seven rivals.
Narrower: only via observables the field does not measure, and full-rank coupling is not ruled out.

3.3 Finetuning selects the bundle

Finetune on one example of one trait and the persona's other traits move, in proportion to p

  • One example of color: blue, a persona-B value. Untouched traits (pet, drink) move toward B's values.
  • Zero OOD shift at p = 0.5; +0.13 at p = 0.9.
  • Emergent misalignment with the cause known: breadth is inherited from pretraining, not from the finetune.

Fig. 2.9

3.3 Finetuning selects the bundle

Spillover, the trained value leaking everywhere, depends on the learning rate and on nothing else

  • At 10⁻³ the value leaks in every cell; at 10⁻⁴ nowhere. p plays no role.
  • The persona shift appears at all learning rates. So the two are different phenomena, and this learning rate is the chapter's recurring regime boundary.
  • Timing, softly: the persona shift lags the in-distribution fit by roughly 2× (half-change at 9 vs 4 steps). Plot in backup.

Fig. 2.11

3.3 Finetuning selects the bundle · mechanism

On the traits the finetune never touched, ~⅘ of the change is the prior over personas moving. On the trained trait itself, it is the conditional

ΔL = Σ Δq ℓ + Σ q Δℓ + …prior channel + conditional channel (+ interaction, residual)
  • Left panel, untouched traits: prior channel dominates (0.82–0.85) at selection-regime learning rates.
  • Right panel, the trained trait: conditional channel dominates (0.99).
  • So: the side effects are selection; the intended effect is modification. At the hot learning rate the left panel flips too, which is what spillover is.

Fig. 2.12

3.3 Finetuning selects the bundle · P1 splits

The model's belief over three personas sits on a triangle in activation space. Finetuning slides the belief point along it without bending the triangle. But the probe direction is not where the movement happens

  • Belief point moves 178× more than the triangle deforms at cold learning rates; only 13× at hot ones.
  • Patching null: put the pre-finetune value back along the probe direction and the OOD shift reverts ~0%. Put back the whole activation and it reverts 100%.

Reading a direction and acting through it are different claims. This is where P1 splits, before Ch. 3 meets it at scale.

3.4 Framing redirects selection (P3)

Prepend a persona-consistent cue to the same finetuning example and ~80% of the side effect disappears. Inoculation prompting in miniature

  • pet: dog . already identifies persona B, so blue carries no evidence about who is speaking. There is nothing to move onto the prior.
  • Off-grammar labels (persona: B ., persona: A .) cut a third, whichever persona they name. The cue must be semantically live.

3.5 How far does the model predict?

The problem: before running a finetune, predict how large its side effects will be, using only the pretrained checkpoint

  • Given: the corpus's p, the pretraining budget, the finetuning learning rate, and cheap probes of the checkpoint. One of them, grad_proj_cos, asks how much the very first gradient step already points along the transfer direction.
  • Predict: the whole OOD-shift curve over 100 finetuning steps, for a fresh model at an unseen (p, budget) cell, on a trait never used in calibration.
  • Score: R² of predicted vs realized curve.

How it was run252-model calibration grid. Then a competing fleet submitted 147 predictors against sealed held-out cells; 80 were scored. The reported law is the best of 80, not the first that worked.

OOD(t) = A · G(t)a shared shape × a per-cell amplitude

3.5 How far does the model predict?

Across cells, every persona observable tracks transfer size. Within a cell only the gradient projection tells seeds apart. No predictor beat R² = 0 on held-out trajectories

  • Seed-level: gradient projection r = +0.41; ICL score r = −0.06.
  • Held-out trajectory R² = −1.38, vs −21 for the trivial predictor. An order of magnitude better, still negative, for all 147 attempts.

Lawful in expected magnitude, unpredictable in individual trajectory. The collapse onto one shape holds cold and breaks hot (backup).

Fig. 2.15

3.5 How far does the model predict? · RL

KL-regularized RL has a known optimum: tilt the reference policy by the reward. Under the mixture model, that tilt predicts bundle transfer with no fitted constants

π* ∝ π0 · er/βKorbak et al.: KL-RL is Bayesian inference. Substitute the mixture for π₀ and the persona posterior tilts in closed form.
  • Reward = 1 for persona B's value of one trait. Prediction for the untouched traits drawn before the trainer existed: 375 numbers committed to git.

ResultThe optimizer reaches the optimum (KL to π* ≤ 5×10⁻⁴ nats). Transfer to untouched traits is a median 24% of the prediction. Not the optimizer, not the readout, not the endpoint: three controls.

3.5 How far does the model predict? · RL

The optimum fixes the product of persona weights and conditionals, not the split. Gradient descent takes the cheap route and moves the factor that does not carry

  • The tilt asks the persona posterior to move by 0.18–0.40; it moves by 0.04, flat in β and p.
  • Re-scoping, not refutation: the tilt is an upper bound on transfer. The realized fraction is an optimizer property.
  • Inverted prediction: SFT routes more through the prior than RL does (0.47 vs 0.27).

3.6 Geometry and curriculum, briefly

How many independent persona factors can a corpus carry? Bounded above by algebra (r ≤ 1/ρ) and below by what a model can learn, and the bounds nearly meet

  • Low correlation is needed for rank, but low correlation means little evidence per behavior. Below ~0.3 nats nothing is learned.
  • Width helps by buying optimization budget, not capacity: a 256-d model matches the exact reasoner at matched evidence. Details in backup.

3.6 Geometry and curriculum, briefly

Two curriculum knobs, two quantities: when persona documents arrive sets availability; how personas are interleaved sets separability

  • Late placement pushes availability past a Bayes ceiling that is placement-invariant by construction: the model is recency-biased. Separability flat.
  • Staged composition raises gradient separability ~1.6× and halves transfer, at the cost of in-context flexibility.
