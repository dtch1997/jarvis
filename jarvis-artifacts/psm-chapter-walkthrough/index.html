PhD thesis · Chapter 2

The Persona Selection Model

Models internalize the trait bundles of their training data as latent personas. Behavior under prompting and training is governed by which persona the evidence selects.

Daniel Tan · UCL · supervisory meeting 2026-09-14

Summary

Five claims, each with a ceiling

The latent variable is real.

A blind analyst names the generative world from behavior alone. But one rival, full-rank coupling, is never separated.

Finetuning selects.

The prior over personas carries ~⅘ of generalization. But the probe that reads the prior is not the axis it moves along.

Selection is lawful in magnitude.

Side-effect size is readable off the checkpoint. But individual trajectories are not, and the parameter-free RL tilt is only a bound.

Persona space has geometry.

Distance, hierarchy, depth and order each govern a measurable quantity. But each law stops at a boundary, and rank is squeezed.

The curriculum is a lever on selectivity, with two knobs.

Composition raises separability and halves transfer; placement moves availability. But separability could not be moved apart from coupling: one shared pathway, not two.

All of it is toy scale: ~1M-parameter transformers on synthetic corpora with known latent structure. Nothing here is a measurement about a frontier model.

Part 1

A latent-variable model of personas

Where the idea comes from, what it has to explain, which statistical models are a priori plausible, and the four predictions the thesis commits to.

1.1 Why personas

An LLM is not one author. It is a simulator of the many authors who wrote its training data

  • To predict text written by many sources, the model must infer who is writing and continue as them.
  • A persona is the bundle of traits one source carries. The "assistant" is one character the simulator can play.

Andreas (2022), Language Models as Agent Models, Findings of EMNLP · janus (2022), Simulators, LessWrong · Shanahan, McDonell & Reynolds (2023), Role play with large language models, Nature 623 · Anthropic (2026), The Persona Selection Model

1.1 Why personas · in their words

The framing is not new. What is new is making it predictive

Andreas 2022 · Findings of EMNLP"When performing next word prediction given a textual context, an LM can infer and represent properties of an agent likely to have produced that context."

janus 2022 · Simulators, LessWrong"In the agentic AI ontology, there is no difference between the policy and the effective agent, but for GPT, there is."

Shanahan, McDonell & Reynolds 2023 · Nature"We can think of an LLM as a non-deterministic simulator capable of role-playing an infinity of characters … the dialogue agent maintains a superposition of simulacra that are consistent with the preceding context."

Anthropic 2026 · The Persona Selection Model"An accurate enough autocomplete engine must learn to simulate the human-like characters appearing in text—real people, fictional characters, sci-fi robots, and so forth."

All four re-describe behavior after the fact. None commits to predictions about training dynamics, and none is scored against a catalogue it did not choose. That is the gap this chapter fills.

1.3 Candidate statistical models

Nine ways behaviors could come to correlate: a hidden cause, direct couplings, or copied exemplars

  • Left: a latent variable explains the correlations. Split by whether it takes finitely many values (personas) or varies continuously.
  • Middle: no latent; behaviors pull on each other directly.
  • Right: no structure at all, just a bank of copied documents.
  • Two identities collapse the picture: low-rank couplings are factor models, and admixture becomes a mixture as its blending vanishes.

1.3 Candidate statistical models

The field's standard measurement, "train on A, how much does B move", cannot distinguish any of these

response of B to a small push on A = Cov(A, B)Lemma 1. Single-behavior transfer, single-behavior conditionals and within-document agreement are all functions of the corpus's first two moments.
  • Any two structures with the same pairwise correlations give identical transfer numbers. Verified numerically across all twelve fielded worlds.
  • What can discriminate: how a behavior responds as evidence about several other behaviors accumulates. Every discriminating result in the chapter is of that kind.

1.3 Candidate statistical models

Scored on the regularities, four structures survive. Among them, the chapter commits to an atomic prior as a modelling choice

Hierarchical mixture5survives
Multi-factor5survives
Admixture5survives
Retrieval4survives
DAG · Ising · max-ent · IRT≤ 2eliminated

Low-rank couplings turn out to be factor models (Hubbard–Stratonovich), so the survivors differ mainly in the shape of the prior over the latent: a finite set of personas, or a continuum.

Modelling choiceReal persona structure is neither purely atomic nor purely smooth. The chapter commits to atoms, finitely many personas, because that is what makes "selection" a well-defined operation and lets every toy world carry an exact posterior.

The escalation curve (how consistently a held-out behavior follows k consistent ones) is the measurement that would locate a real model between the two limits. Registered; unrun.

1.4 The model

Behavior is a mixture over personas; training reweights the mixture

πθ(y | x) ≈ Σz qθ(z | x) · πθ(y | z, x)q = posterior over which persona is speaking · π(y|z,x) = persona-conditioned behavior
  • Prompting moves the posterior q without touching θ.
  • Training upweights personas under which the data is probable, and drags along every trait they carry.

Two corollariesOvergeneralization by default: narrow training selects a whole persona (Ch. 4, emergent misalignment).
Controllability: framing that is inert for the behavior still changes which persona explains it (Ch. 5, inoculation).

Scope: "reweights" describes the regime where pretraining dominates. Post-training can in principle create personas; the chapter does not claim otherwise.

1.4 The model

Four predictions written in advance. Two hold with a condition, one is open diagnostically, P1 splits

PredictedOutcome
P1Traits are directions in activation space; intervening on them moves behaviorSplit. Reading holds (probe decodes the prior at rank corr. 1.000). Acting fails: patching the direction reverts ~0% of the shift.
P2Narrow finetuning shifts the persona's other traits, tracking pretraining correlationSupported, bounded. Magnitude predictable; individual trajectory not; RL tilt over-predicts ~4×.
P3Equivalent framings of the same data generalize differentlySupported, with a condition. Only when the framing is semantically live for the model.
P4Separability, set by the curriculum, gates how selectively a persona can be targetedSupported causally. Mediator collinear with coupling; two causal measures predict transfer, the gradient cosine does not.

Part 1 · tl;dr

Part 1 in four lines

1An LLM simulates the many authors of its training data. A persona is one author's bundle of traits.
2Persona selection offers a compact hypothesis for ten empirical phenomena.
3Persona selection can be operationalised as a latent-variable generative model.

CaveatLine 4 is an empirical regime, not a law. Nothing forbids post-training from adding new personas; if most compute went to post-training it plainly would. The claim holds where pretraining dominates, which is the regime studied here.

Claim 1 · the latent is real · tournament

The tournament: eight worlds, one blind classifier

Claim 1 · the latent is real · tournament

The mixture is named blind, 5 out of 5

  • What is classified: each held-out trained model, from a vector of behavioral measurements (escalation curve and its ceiling, response-matrix rank and asymmetry, probe persistence). No pairwise statistics. Logistic regression, not a single probe.
  • Reading the plot: rows = the world the model was actually trained on, columns = the classifier's call, five held-out models per row. Accuracy 0.575 vs chance 0.125.
  • Both mixture rows sit entirely on the diagonal. The coupling worlds blur into each other; full-rank Ising is always called DAG.
  • Nine of ten pre-registered sub-predictions about where confusions land missed. The one hit: corpus statistics alone cannot predict transfer size.

Claim 1 · readable, not steerable

A probe reads the persona prior perfectly, but restoring it reverts none of the side effect

  • Probe: a linear map from the pretrained model's residual stream to P(persona), fit once and frozen. It tracks exact Bayes at rank correlation 1.00.
  • Patch: after finetuning, overwrite the finetuned model's activation along that one direction with its pre-finetune value. The side effect on untouched traits reverts ~0%. Overwrite the whole activation instead: 100%.
  • So the direction that decodes the prior is not the direction finetuning moves it along. The control shows the patching machinery works.
  • One reading: the persona lives in a low-rank subspace of k directions. Any one of them is enough to read it; steering needs all k. Untested here: patch the top-k probe subspace and see whether reversion rises with k.

P1 split in half. Ch. 3 asks the same question of steering vectors at scale.

Claim 2 · mechanism

Every change in behavior splits into "which persona is speaking" and "what that persona does"

ΔL = Σz Δq(z) ℓ(z) + Σz q(z) Δℓ(z) + Δq·Δℓ + residualprior channel: the mixture weights moved · conditional channel: a persona's own behavior moved · interaction · residual = how far the model departs from the mixture form
  • L = behavior score on a trait (logit difference toward persona B's value).
  • q(z) = P(persona z), read at the persona: field.
  • ℓ(z) = the same behavior score with the context pinned to persona z (all other traits set to z's values, posterior 0.99 on z).

Why this is measurable hereThe corpus grammar exposes q directly, and the pinned prefixes expose ℓ. Measure both before and after the finetune; the identity is exact algebra, so the four terms account for the whole change.

Measured on five traits, three p, five seeds, four finetuning learning rates.

Claim 2 · mechanism

Side effects move the prior; the trained trait moves the conditional

  • Reading the plot: each bar is one finetuning learning rate. Its height is split by how much of the total change (sum of the four terms' magnitudes) each channel accounts for.
  • Left, untouched traits: the prior channel (blue) is ~80% at cold learning rates. The side effect is the mixture weights moving.
  • Right, the finetuned trait: the conditional channel (orange) dominates. The trained behavior is direct modification. The grey residual there says part of it lies below the persona abstraction.
  • At 10⁻³ the left panel flips to conditional: spillover is modification.

Claim 2 · in a hierarchy

Personas in families: do side effects leak to a sibling?

  • Four personas on a tree: two families (A, B), each with two subtypes (A1, A2, B1, B2). Two subtypes of the same family are siblings: A1 and A2 share the family-A traits but never appear in the same document. One persona per document, as before.
  • A private trait is a trait that belongs to one subtype. Its sibling shows it at the family rate pF; the other family never does. That shared rate is the only link between siblings.
  • Prediction: training A1's private trait moves A2's, because the cheapest explanation of the datum is "this is family A".

Claim 2 · in a hierarchy

Finetuning a subtype moves its sibling

  • Reading the plot: y = change in probability of the aligned value on each tier after finetuning one A1 private trait. Solid = model; hatched = what an exact Bayesian would do given the same corpus.
  • Same leaf, parent and sibling all rise by about +0.11: the whole family A moves as one unit. Family B falls, as it must in a closed two-family simplex. The subtype axis, the true null, stays at zero.
  • Turn pF down to 0.5 and the sibling shift goes to zero: the leakage runs through the parent.

Future work · quantitative laws

Can side effects be predicted before the finetune runs?

  • Given: the pretrained checkpoint and cheap probes of it. One probe asks how much the first gradient step already points along the transfer direction.
  • Predict: the whole side-effect curve of a fresh model on an unseen trait.
  • 147 competing predictors; the best of 80 scored is reported.

Future work · quantitative laws

Size is predictable; the exact trajectory is not

  • Across cells, every persona observable tracks how big the side effect will be. Within a cell, only the gradient projection tells seeds apart (r = +0.41).
  • Held-out trajectory R² = −1.38: ten times better than the trivial predictor, still negative, for all 147 attempts.

Fig. 2.15 · shape collapse in backup

Future work · quantitative laws

The RL tilt is an upper bound, not a prediction

π* ∝ π0 · er/βKL-regularized RL has a closed-form optimum: tilt the reference policy by the reward. Under the mixture model that tilt predicts side effects with zero fitted constants.
  • The policy reaches the optimum. Side effects are a median 24% of the prediction (dashed).
  • Why: the optimum fixes the product of persona weights and conditionals, not the split. Gradient descent moves the cheap factor, the one that does not carry.

Fig. 2.17

Future work · the curriculum lever (P4)

When data arrives sets availability; how it is mixed sets separability

  • Late placement of persona documents raises availability past a placement-invariant Bayes ceiling; separability flat.
  • Staged composition raises separability ~1.6× and halves transfer, at the cost of in-context coupling.

Future work · geometry of persona space

Persona space has geometry

Distance lawIn-context transfer decays with correlation-graph distance at exactly the Bayes slope; finetuning transfer ignores distance.
Installed defaultsPost-training installs a default whose in-context basin is deeper than Bayes, and that depth buys no protection against erosion.
Rank squeezeA corpus can carry stable rank at most 1/ρ, and needs enough evidence per behavior to be learnable. The two bounds nearly meet at toy scale.
Self-descriptive editingTraining on the model's own name moves 32 unseen behaviors in the pattern the exact posterior predicts; a matched control does not.
Separability vs couplingFive further curricula, 120 cells: transfer follows in-context coupling and ignores separability. One shared pathway, not two.

Figures in backup. The pattern across all of them: geometry predicts what a model computes on a context, not what a gradient step drags along.
